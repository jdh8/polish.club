# Multi 2♦

Multi 2♦ shows a 6-card weak two of either major.

| 2♦-  | PRE, 6+♠♥ |
|------|-----------|
| 2♥♠! | P/C
| 2NT! | (R) INV+
| 3♣♦  | NAT F
| 3♥♠! | P/C
| 3NT  | S/O
| 4♣!  | STR COG
| 4♦!  | PRE COG
| 4♥♠  | S/O

There are two common approaches to rebid after the 2NT inquiry:

- Bisect strength and keep all contracts right-sided.
- Trisect strength but allow 3♥♠ to be wrong-sided.

I prefer trisection because it allows accurate description of a wild 2♦ opening.

| 2♦-2NT- | INV+ |
|---------|------|
| 3♣!     | INV+, 6+♥
| 3♦!     | INV+, 6+♠
| 3♥      | MIN, 6+♥
| 3♠      | MIN, 6+♠

There are two kinds of choice of games:

- **4♣** by strength
- **4♦** by distribution

Such distinction helps judging how to react to interference.  Besides, 4♣ lets
the strong hand declare.  On the other hand, 4♦ closes the auction ASAP.

| 2♦-4♣- | STR COG  |
|--------|----------|
| 4♦!    | TRF, 6+♥ |
| 4♥!    | TRF, 6+♠ |

## Competitive bidding

Passing the double clearly shows a willingness to play in 2♦x.  The question is
how to use XX.  I recommend using XX to complement 2♥♠ and make efficient use of
bidding space.  This premise narrows the options down to two promising treatments:

- XX asks for opener's long major, while 2♥♠ are natural.
- XX asks for opener's **short** major, while 2♥♠ retain their P/C meanings.

I prefer the latter, as the former leaves the meaning of 3♥♠ unclear.

| 2♦(X) | - |
|-------|---|
| XX!   | Ask for the **short** major
| 2♥♠!  | P/C
