# Checkback Gladiator

Checkback Gladiator is our name for a variant of [XYZ][xyz], the two-way
checkback in which 2♣ puppets to 2♦ and 2♦ is an artificial game force.  The
version here was taught at BTU as *two-way puppet checkback*, [documented on
PTT as early as 1999][ptt].  It applies after 1♣ - 1M - 1♠, 1♦ - 1M - 1♠, and
every 1x - 1M - 1NT, with the auction-specific tables in [1♣ - 1M][1c],
[1♦ - 1M][1d], and [1♥ - 1♠][1h].

There are 3 artificial relays in Checkback Gladiator:

- **2♣:** several variants:
  - to play 2x
  - strongly shaped invitation
  - balanced invitation
  - special game force, defined per auction
- **2♦:** game-forcing checkback
- **2NT:** transfer to clubs

As a corollary, natural 2x are wide-range invitations and 3x become slam tries.
Compared with XYZ in literature, direct 2x are swapped with the variants in 2♣.
This is because invitations are prone to competition, so they take the direct
route; a weak hand can afford to sign off a round later.

## Why Gladiator rather than Stayman

The name follows 1NT continuations.  *Checkback* is short for *Checkback
Stayman*: the original 2♣ asked opener about the majors and opener answered
naturally, as over a 1NT opening.  XYZ's 2♣ asks nothing.  It puppets to 2♦
so that responder can sign off or invite, and the game-forcing enquiry moves
to 2♦.  That is exactly [Gladiator over 1NT][glad]: 2♣ puppets to 2♦ to carry
weak and invitational hands, and 2♦ is game-forcing Stayman.  A relay that
contains weak continuations is a Gladiator, not a Stayman.

[glad]: https://www.bridgehands.com/G/Gladiator.htm

## History

Gladiator is the older idea.  It is [reported to appear in the first edition
of Roman Club][bbo] in the 1950s, where Belladonna preferred one negative and
three positives at the 2 level to Stayman's three negatives and one positive.
The same count holds here: 2♣ is the only rebid that may be weak, and 2♦,
2♥, and 2♠ are all positive.  Two-way checkback itself is much younger.  [A
Bridge Winners search for its origin][bw] traces it to Cowan in *The Bridge
World*, 1988, and the name XYZ to Joe Kivel in *Bridge Today*.
