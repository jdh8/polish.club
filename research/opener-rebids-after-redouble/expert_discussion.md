# Expert discussion and theory: opener's actions after 1x (X) XX

Scope: argued theory and debate (articles, columns, forums) on what opener does after responder redoubles a takeout double, with attention to 1♣. This file compares the sources with the book's rule for 1♣ (X) XX: "Forcing pass: catchall takeout. Double: penalty. Bid 2♣ or below: to compete. Cuebid: GF two-suited takeout. 3NT: 15–17. ## Q1. Is opener's pass forcing after 1x (X) XX (bid)? What does an immediate bid by opener show?

### Takeaway
The mainstream view is that opener's pass after XX and an escape is forcing at low levels. Opener's immediate bid in front of the expected penalty double shows a weak, shapely minimum ("unsuitable for defence"), not extra values. Extra values are shown by passing first and bidding later ("pass-and-pull"). Bridge World Standard makes the pass forcing "everywhere". Several experts cap the force at about 2♥ or "below two of the opened suit", and some modern players dislike forcing passes altogether.

### Cited Findings
- Bridge World Standard (complete system text): "After one of a suit–(double)–redouble–(bid)–?, opener's pass is forcing everywhere." Also: "After one of a suit–(double)–redouble–(pass)–pass–(bid)–?, responder's pass is forcing everywhere." And: "A nonjump, nonreverse new-suit bid by opener is nonforcing." — [BWS complete system, bridgeworld.com](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- BWS general default: "When a forcing bid is doubled and there is no contrary explicit system agreement or logic from the auction, a pass is forcing and a redouble is to play (suggests a contract)." Also: "(a) when a pass would be forcing, a double discourages further offensive bidding" (this is for doubles of our forcing bids, a different context). — [BWS complete system](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- David Lindop, "What's Standard? The Redouble — Part I" (Audrey Grant's *Better Bridge*, Sept/Oct 2008): "Since responder has not yet had a chance to describe the hand other than it contains 10 or more high-card points, opener usually passes, waiting to hear what responder says next. The only exceptions are when opener has a weak, distributional hand, or when advancer bids and opener wants to double to suggest defending for penalty." His summary box: "Pass, with most hands. / Bid, with a weak, distributional hand. / Double advancer's bid for penalty." — [Better Bridge PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Lindop, same article. On 1♠ (X) XX (2♦) with ♠KQ98752 ♥84 ♦3 ♣KQ4, bid 2♠: "We've opened 1♠ with a weak, distributional hand and don't want to defend for penalty even if partner doubles 2♦. So, we let partner know the nature of our hand right away." With ♠AQJ92 ♥AK863 ♦7 ♣J5, pass: "We have a good hand and don't intend to defend for penalty if partner doubles. But if we bid now, we show a weak hand. By passing, and then bidding later, we show a sound opening, with the values for at least game opposite responder's 10+ points." — [Better Bridge PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Larry Cohen, "LC Standard — Over Opp's Takeout Double" (Bridge Winners, 15 July 2012): "If the opener runs prematurely, he is showing a minimum, shapely hand." Example 1♠–X–XX–2♣–2♠: "2♠ is showing a minimum opener with 6 or more spades." — [Bridge Winners](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/lc-standard-over-opps-takeout-double/); same content at [larryco.com](https://www.larryco.com/bridge-articles/over-opps-to-double)
- Bob Crosby, "Forcing Passes Revisited" (2003), on forcing passes switched on by a strength redouble: "Bidding in front of the impending double means that you are weak and distributional"; "Passing and pulling the double is never done from a position of weakness … you would have bid already so it is a slam try"; "The pass means I am getting out of the way for you to double and am happy to defend." His example auction is 1♣–P–1♦–X–XX–1♠, not the exact 1x (X) XX. — [ACBL Unit 390 page](https://www.acblunit390.org/Simon/fp.htm)
- Bob Mackinnon, "The Puppet Pass" (2012): "The simplest example of a forcing pass is the pass by the opening bidder after a redouble by partner." Also: "If opener bids a suit immediately it is non-forcing, leaving further action to the redoubler." His "puppet pass" idea: opener passes "to ask partner to double unless he has an exceptional hand," then will "pass or bid a suit, forcing, to show a good hand unsuitable for a penalty." And: "We treat 'pass-and-pull' as strong." — [bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Bobby Wolff, Aces on Bridge, Sunday 4 Aug 2013, answering a reader who asked whether 1-suit (X) XX sets up forcing passes: "The redouble forces opener or his partner to bid again at low levels (below two of the opened suit)." He adds (paraphrased by the fetch tool) that the opponents cannot be allowed to play two clubs undoubled in the reader's auction, and that an exception might apply after a third-seat opening. — [aces.bridgeblogging.com ?p=6299](https://aces.bridgeblogging.com/?p=6299); also at [2013/08/18 permalink](https://aces.bridgeblogging.com/2013/08/18/the-aces-on-bridge-sunday-august-4th-2013/)
- George Cuppaidge, "Making 'Redouble' Work for You" (CSB News, 21 Mar 2016, from QBA bulletin June 2014), boxed principle: "Following a redouble, when partner's bid of one of a suit is doubled, passes are forcing and doubles are for penalty." He also wants the redouble to show 12+. — [csbnews.org](https://csbnews.org/en/making-redouble-work-for-you-by-george-cuppaidge)
- Mats Nilsland, "Penalty-Oriented Redoubles" (Bridge Winners, 1 Mar 2022), gives an upper limit: an "unwritten rule that we shouldn't let the opponents play any undoubled contract below 2♥." On 1♥ X XX 2♠: "If South passes, it isn't forcing." He adds that at a low level "we may set an upper limit (in the case above the upper limit is 2♥)." — [Bridge Winners](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/penalty-oriented-redoubles/)
- Nilsland, "Penalty double and forcing pass agreements" (Bridge Winners, 12 Mar 2022): partnerships must decide "which redoubles and to what level"; one candidate agreement is "Double always creates a forcing pass to the level of X." — [Bridge Winners](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/penalty-double-and-forcing-pass-agreements/)
- BBO forum, "opponents escape 1NT redoubled, are we in forcing pass?". This is a 1NT context, but the principles transfer. Zelandakh: "Almost everyone for whom I have found agreements on this plays a forcing pass over 2m and no FP over 2♠"; 2♥ is "the tricky case." CSGibson: "X is penalty, pass is forcing up to 2♥." mfa1010: "If they bid to the 3-level then I would assume the force is off." gszes argues the opposite, that there is "no reason a FP situation ceases to be forcing just because the suit has changed." — [BBO 53068](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/53068-opponents-escape-1nt-redoubled-are-we-in-forcing-pass/)
- Objections to forcing-pass agreements (BBO "Forcing Pass" thread). helene_t: "Many pairs also play certain passes as forcing after we double their 1NT or when we make a business redouble" (she personally dislikes it). DavidKok: "Forcing Pass can very easily become a blame transfer." mikeh: "Simple rules may not be optimal, in theory, but are often" the practical choice. — [BBO 88212](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/88212-forcing-pass)
- Pavlicek's system notes: "A prerequisite for any competitive double is that we are not in a force, i.e., we have not redoubled or otherwise indicated partnership assets of 23+ HCP where a pass would be forcing." The redouble over 1♣ (X) is "10+ HCP, implies no fit, creates force." — [rpbridge.net 7g01](https://www.rpbridge.net/7g01.htm)

### Inferences
- Under the expert consensus, the book's "Forcing pass: catchall takeout" agrees with BWS and the Lindop/Crosby/Mackinnon school on the *forcing* part. Its label is unusual, though. Standard sources describe opener's forcing pass as "waiting / happy to defend / let partner double" (Lindop, Crosby), not as a takeout call. The book's reading, where pass is takeout and double is penalty, matches Nilsland's "reversed penalty doubles" framing and the BWS idea that a pass in a forcing situation invites partner's decision.
- The book's "Bid 2♣ or below: to compete" matches the expert consensus that an immediate bid is weak/shapely, "unsuitable for defence" (Lindop, Cohen, Crosby, Mackinnon: "non-forcing").
- The book's "Other: 18+, natural" for higher bids conflicts with the standard idea that a jump or bid in front of the double shows weakness or shape. But for a Polish 1♣ (which includes 18+ strong hands), opener has no other way to show the strong variant cheaply. Under the standard approach, strength is shown by pass-then-bid (Lindop: "values for at least game"; Crosby: slam try). The book trades that for immediate natural strong bids.
- For a 1♣ opening specifically, Wolff's "below two of the opened suit" limit would make the force cover only 1-level escapes (1♦/1♥/1♠). Nilsland's "below 2♥" and Zelandakh's "over 2m" limits would cover 2♣ and 2♦ escapes as well. If the book wants the force to cover 2♦, it should say so explicitly.

### Gaps

- No source specifically discusses opener's forcing pass after **1♣** (X) XX in a strong/Polish club context.

## Q2. Is opener's double of LHO's escape penalty or takeout/cooperative?

### Takeaway
The classical and textbook answer is "all doubles after our redouble are penalty" (Cohen, Lindop, Cuppaidge; BWS treats the redouble-then-double as a penalty suggestion). A significant minority of strong players use "cooperative" or "cards" doubles (Pavlicek, Mackinnon). Others have switched to takeout doubles at low levels, with the penalty pass replacing the penalty double.

### Cited Findings
- Larry Cohen: "After our redouble, any subsequent doubles are penalty." — [Bridge Winners LC Standard](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/lc-standard-over-opps-takeout-double/). Also: "After this redouble, all further doubles would be for penalty." — [larryco.com Over Opponent's Takeout Double](https://larryco.com/bridge-articles/over-opponents-takeout-double)
- Lindop. On 1♠ (X) XX (2♦) with ♠AK862 ♥82 ♦QJ105 ♣A3, double: "Here we have an excellent hand if partner is interested in defending. We can let partner know we have length and strength in diamonds. If we pass, partner might have cards in hearts and clubs but not want to defend if the opponents have found a good fit. Partner doesn't have to pass the double with other things in mind." — [Better Bridge PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- BWS: "Responder's redouble followed by a penalty double is a weaker penalty suggestion" than a penalty double made after responder first passes the double. — [BWS complete system](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Pavlicek, cooperative doubles: "A cooperative double is a penalty suggestion at the 2 or 3 level." His list of cooperative cases includes "After partner redoubled or made a force-double." — [rpbridge.net 7g01](https://www.rpbridge.net/7g01.htm)
- Mackinnon, on opener's direct double after XX and a 2♣ escape: "I don't have a good double of clubs, but you can pass if your hand is largely defensive." — [The Puppet Pass](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Nilsland on "reversed penalty doubles": the forcing pass "can be thought of as reversed penalty doubles, as we then must double to enable partner to make a penalty pass." He notes the cost: you may be unable to double when you both have length in their suit. — [Bridge Winners Penalty-Oriented Redoubles](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/penalty-oriented-redoubles/)
- BBO (1NT XX escape context). Phil: "1st double cards, 2nd double takeout." Zelandakh: "It is very common to play the first double in these auctions as takeout," though "the standard without discussion is still penalty." mycroft: they "play penalty doubles after the redouble, but probably should switch to takeout." P_Marlowe: default forcing-pass agreements "would make X penalty," but his partnership switched to takeout doubles at low levels. CSGibson: doubling "behind the person showing the suit is penalty, in front cooperative." — [BBO 53068](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/53068-opponents-escape-1nt-redoubled-are-we-in-forcing-pass/)
- Bob Crosby: "Doubling by yourself is self evident" (that is, penalty within a forcing-pass structure). — [ACBL Unit 390](https://www.acblunit390.org/Simon/fp.htm)

### Inferences
- The book's "Double: penalty" is the classical default, backed by Cohen, Lindop, Cuppaidge and Crosby, and by BWS's penalty framing.
- The book's pairing of "forcing pass = catchall takeout" with "double = penalty" is internally coherent. Pass carries the takeout and "no clear direction" hands, so double can stay pure. This is the reverse of the modern trend some BBO experts describe, where double is takeout or cards and pass is a penalty-oriented waiting move. The trade-off is Nilsland's: with a pure penalty double, opener can't show a "cards but not trumps" hand except by passing.
- Seat matters for opener's double. In 1♣ (X) XX (bid), the escaper is the advancer, who is opener's RHO. Opener therefore acts directly *behind* the escaper's suit, while the redoubler sits in front of it. Under CSGibson's heuristic ("behind … penalty, in front cooperative"), opener's double is the penalty one. That supports the book's "Double: penalty". A later double by the redoubler, sitting in front of the suit, would be the natural candidate for a cooperative meaning.

### Gaps
- No Bridge World Master Solvers' Club problem on opener's double after 1x (X) XX was found online; the MSC archives are mostly paywalled.
- No source discusses doubles after 1♣ (X) XX when 1♣ may be a 2-card or artificial suit.

## Q3. After 1x (X) XX (P): when should opener pull or bid? Is pass "to play" standard?

### Takeaway
After 1x (X) XX (P), standard textbooks say opener passes with a normal opening. The doubler, not opener, is the one under pressure, because 1x redoubled usually makes with large overtricks. Opener bids only with a weak, distributional hand. Theory on the advancer's side holds that advancer's pass of a one-level redouble starts a scramble, so the doubler is expected to run.

### Cited Findings
- Lindop on 1♥ (X) XX (P) with ♠A93 ♥AKJ83 ♦762 ♣J4: "Pass. We have a typical opening. There's no reason to say anything at this point." And: "What if we pass and East also passes? First, that's very unlikely, since East doubled for takeout and is likely very short in hearts. Even if East does decide to pass, we should have little trouble taking at least seven tricks when partner holds 10 or more high-card points … Seven tricks in 1♥ redoubled gives us a game bonus." — [Better Bridge PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Lindop, from the doubler's seat, on 1♠ (X) XX P P ?: "We can't afford to pass, since 1♠ redoubled will likely make with several overtricks. We'll have to start scrambling." — [same](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Lindop Part II (Nov/Dec 2008), example 1♠ X XX P P 2♥ X: "notice how opener passes the auction back to us to give us an opportunity to double for penalty." On redoubler's continuations: after 1♦ X XX 1♠ P P, responder bids "1NT, showing about 10-12 points. This is not forcing." After 1♠ X XX 2♥ P P, responder's 2♠ "is not forcing; it's only invitational. Opener can pass with a minimum," and a jump to 3♠ "would be forcing, showing … about 13 or more points." — [Better Bridge Part II PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200811.pdf)
- Advancer's pass of a one-level redouble. awm: "I think it is fairly standard that a pass of a _one-level_ redouble is the start of a scramble." Jlall: "Pass should be scrambling and always have at least 3 of the higher unbid suit." mikeh dissents: "I personally play that the pass of the redouble is penalty," while conceding "the scramble is a more frequent hand type." neilkaz: "I most definately wouldn't play that pass of a redoubled 5 card major opening shows a desire to defend." — [BBO 17301](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/17301-1h-double-redouble/)
- Grant/Lindop on advancer: "After a redouble, advancer is no longer obliged to bid," but "advancer should bid with a distinct preference for one of the unbid suits." — [Better Bridge PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Ed Judy, "Proceeding after 1m-(Dbl)-ReDbl-(Pass or 1X)" (Bridge Winners, 9 Jan 2018), proposes that after 1m X XX (P or 1X), opener's "1NT rebid specifically shows the equivalent of a weak 12-14 weak 1NT hand" and "any other call -- pass or bid -- denies such a holding." His double-dummy simulation found opener holds a balanced 12–14 in about 58% of these deals. — [Bridge Winners](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/proceeding-after-1m-dbl-redbl-pass-or-1x/)

### Inferences
- For a *natural* 1x opening, passing 1x (X) XX (P) is safe and standard. For a Polish 1♣, which may hold only 2 clubs (the weak-NT variant), letting 1♣XX play is much less attractive. The doubler may hold clubs and still pass, since a takeout double of a Polish 1♣ is often treated loosely. No online source tests this case. Judy's 1m proposal is the closest: a weak-NT 1NT rebid by opener "to describe" rather than pass. That idea maps naturally onto Polish 1♣'s 12–14 balanced variant.
- The book's rules ("Forcing pass", "Bid 2♣ or below: to compete") are written for 1♣ (X) XX (bid). The (P) case appears to need its own decision on whether opener's pass of 1♣XX is "to play". The expert material says yes for a natural suit. For a short club, nothing found supports it.

### Gaps
- No expert source found specifically on letting **1♣** redoubled play when 1♣ is artificial or short.
- Judy's article comments, which reportedly debate his proposal, did not load.

## Q4. Opener's cuebid of the doubler's or escaper's suit, 3NT rebids, and jumps after XX

### Takeaway
Very little argued theory exists online on opener's cuebids, 3NT or jumps after partner's redouble. The standard framework (BWS, Lindop, Cohen) treats opener's *immediate* actions as weak/shapely and routes strong hands through pass-then-bid. On the advancer's side, jumps after XX are preemptive and the cuebid is the "monster" bid.

### Cited Findings
- BWS (our side doubled for takeout, advancer over responder's redouble): "a new-suit jump is preemptive." — [BWS complete system](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Advancer over 1♥ X XX. awm: "The standard meaning is that various jumps are mostly preemptive" and "the cuebid (2♥ here) is the main way to show a strong hand." Jlall: "Jumps are all preemptive after XX, and a jump to 2N is minors. A cue would show a monster." — [BBO 17301](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/17301-1h-double-redouble/)
- Lindop: opener shows game-going strength by "passing, and then bidding later." Crosby: pass-and-pull "is a slam try." Mackinnon: "'pass-and-pull' as strong … we need only to bid 2♦ after the double to force." — [Better Bridge](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf); [Crosby](https://www.acblunit390.org/Simon/fp.htm); [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Lindop Part II (responder's view): with 14 HCP after 1♠ X XX 2♥ P P, responder without a penalty double of hearts should "jump to 3NT" as the safe option. — [Better Bridge Part II PDF](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200811.pdf)

### Inferences
- The book's "Cuebid: GF two-suited takeout, clubs and a new suit" and "3NT: 15–17, to play" have no direct counterpart in the natural-system theory found. In natural methods opener's 3NT after XX is not discussed, because a strong balanced opener would open 1NT/2NT. For Polish 1♣ (which includes 18+ balanced and strong unbalanced hands), these direct strong bids fill a gap that natural systems handle by pass-then-bid. The theory supports the book's choice to give the cuebid a strong meaning (by analogy with the advancer's "cue = monster"). It gives no support for a 15–17 3NT specifically; that is a system-specific design choice.
- Conflict: under BWS/Lindop, opener's immediate jump would most naturally be read as shape or weakness (preemptive-style), the opposite of the book's "Other: 18+, natural." The book should treat this as a deliberate alert-worthy divergence.

### Gaps
- No online expert discussion found of opener's cuebid of the *doubler's* suit (doubler has no suit) or of the escaper's suit after 1x (X) XX (bid).
- No source on opener's 3NT range in this auction.
- The Bridge World MSC archive was not searchable online for such problems.

## Q5. Criticism of "redouble starts the penalty process" and the move to transfer or descriptive redoubles

### Takeaway
Modern expert opinion criticises the old "redouble with every 10+" style. Responders now bid suits directly, or use transfers, and keep XX for defensive hands with no fit, so the XX is more penalty-oriented, not less. A separate school uses XX as a transfer (for example, XX = diamonds after 1♣ X). This gives up the penalty redouble, and with it the forcing-pass and penalty-double structure for opener. Opener's continuations then become accept/super-accept/decline, and forum experts warn that these structures "get very complicated" on opener's rebids.

### Cited Findings
- mikeh (BBO, 2013): "Traditionally, at least in NA, the approach was that one redoubled with all hands of 10+ hcp … experienced players realized that this was inefficient," because "you haven't so much as hinted at your spade suit and now the auction is dangerously high." Instead, "you use redouble to show a hand with interest in doubling them: 10+ hcp, no good fit for partner," and "Many experienced players use transfers of one sort or another." — [BBO 61495](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/61495-opponent-makes-a-takeout-double/)
- Fluffy (BBO, same thread): "redouble shows a balanced/2 suiter hand without support that wants them to play doubled." — [BBO 61495](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/61495-opponent-makes-a-takeout-double/)
- Larry Cohen with a strong spade hand over a double: "I'd rather start to describe my suits than to redouble and risk the auction getting too high." Also: "Some partnerships play the redouble denies support for partner, but I don't feel strongly in this matter." — [larryco.com](https://larryco.com/bridge-articles/over-opponents-takeout-double)
- Pavlicek: over 1♣ (X), redouble is "10+ HCP, implies no fit, creates force." — [rpbridge.net 7g01](https://www.rpbridge.net/7g01.htm)
- Transfers over the double (BBO "Transfer responses in competition"). DavidKok: "If they double, calls starting with redouble are a transfer to the strain above it," but also that "Playing XX as the hand that is comfortable defending, and 1♠ as spades, is likely better," and "The structures easily get very complicated, especially once you consider opener's rebids and the continuations," and transfers "always give up one cheap natural call." mikeh: "If you give up on the penalty redouble, as some but not all transfer methods do"; after a transfer to 1NT, "opener doesn't need to accept if he dislikes notrump." mike777 quotes Chip Martel's card for 1♣-(X): "System on at 1-level, RDBL is 10+." awm argues transfers pay off only when they let opener accept with 2–3 card support or give two ways to raise. — [BBO 91402](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/91402-transfer-responses-in-competition/)
- Larry Cohen's optional (non-"vanilla") transfer scheme over a double of 1♥/1♠: "Redouble= 10+ and exactly 2 card support." — [Bridge Winners LC Standard](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/lc-standard-over-opps-takeout-double/)
- An older BBO poster objects to giving up the penalty redouble: with today's light takeout doubles, "this is not something I would like to lose" (search snippet only; thread not fetched). — [BBO "Transfer Advances"](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/1419-transfer-advances/)
- Club-system transfer XX (search snippets only; not fetched/verified). An "Adventures in Bridge" handout on 1m-X transfers lists 1♣ X XX as a transfer to diamonds, 4+ cards, 6+ points: [bridge-tips.co.il PDF](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1m-X-Transfers.pdf). A Precision 1♣ thread gives one pair's structure over a double as "p = 0-4, XX = 5-7, 1♦ = hearts", and says that opener, if not forcing to game, either accepts the transfer or bids two of a higher suit (forcing one round), with "acceptance is nf and tends not to show fit": [BBO 87180](https://www1.dal09.sl.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/87180-inverted-t-walsh/page__view__new)
- Nilsland notes transfers after 1♠ X as "a popular treatment." — [Bridge Winners Penalty-Oriented Redoubles](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/penalty-oriented-redoubles/)

### Inferences
- The critique of "redouble = any 10+" pushes XX *toward* penalty orientation (no fit, defensive). That strengthens rather than weakens the book's premise that "XX starts the penalty process", provided responder bids suits or fits directly with offensive hands.
- If XX becomes a transfer (for example, XX = hearts or diamonds over 1♣ X, as the repo's recent commits explored), opener's forcing-pass / penalty-double framework no longer applies. Opener's actions become transfer acceptance (pass = ? / accept / super-accept / decline). Expert warnings (DavidKok, mikeh) single out exactly this rebid layer as the complexity cost. The penalty hand must then go through another call (often pass-then-double, or a 1NT transfer) and loses the 10+ "our hand" signal.

### Gaps
- No Bridge Winners article specifically debates penalty XX vs. transfer XX over a double of a *strong/Polish* 1♣. BW comment threads (where such debates usually live) could not be loaded.
- Kokish, Robson/Segal, Bergen and Rosenberg writings on this topic were not found online in fetchable form.
