# Opener's continuations after 1-suit (X) XX in natural systems

Scope: natural (non-strong-club) sources only. None of the sources I found gives a separate rule for 1♣. They all state one rule for "one of a suit". The only 1♣/minor distinction I found is in how BWS 2017 defines the XX itself (minor vs. major opening, see §1).

## 1. Expert standard after 1x (X) XX (2y): is pass forcing, is a direct bid weak, is double penalty?

### Takeaway
The published sources agree on the core structure. After 1x (X) XX (bid), opener's pass is forcing (BWS 2017: "forcing everywhere"; Todd; Mackinnon/Kantar). Opener's immediate non-jump bid shows a weak, shapely hand that is unsuitable for defence, and it is non-forcing (Lindop, Cohen, Todd, BWS, Mackinnon). Doubles by either partner after the XX are penalty (Cohen, Walker, Todd, Lindop, Kantar as reported by Mackinnon). BWS 2017 makes opener's double, with a forcing pass available, "discourage further offensive bidding", which is penalty-ish rather than takeout. Only some forum experts treat the first double as takeout or cooperative, and Mackinnon makes the same case.

### Cited Findings
**Meaning of XX**
- BWS 2017 (5th edition, used from January 2017 MSC). After a minor opening: "a redouble shows any hand with 10-plus HCP that is not suitable for a raise or a new-suit bid" (§V.E(g)). After a major opening it is described as "a redouble shows any other hand-type with 10-plus HCP". The fetch tool gave this as §V.D(f), then said on re-check that V.D has no item (f), so the exact section number is unverified. — [BWS 2017 complete system](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Lindop: "When opener's bid of one-of-a-suit is doubled, a redouble by responder shows 10 or more high-card points." "A redouble sends the message to opener: 'This is our hand!'" After redoubling, responder plans to "Double the opponents for penalty", "Bid a new suit (forcing)", "Support opener's suit", or "Bid notrump". — [Better Bridge, Sep/Oct 2008, "The Redouble — Part I"](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Todd: "partner shows a good hand -- 10+ points, and usually no large fit for our suit since partner could bid Jordan 2NT with a large fit". "Most partnerships have the agreement that this redouble sets up a penalty double auction. If the opponents bid a suit and either of us double from then on, it is a penalty double." — [Adventures in Bridge, TWiB (145) "Bidding After Responder Redoubles"](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5127d3d2e4b0b304f0b6db24/t/58dd43f8c534a52312bb72c5/1490895865693/%28145%29+Bidding+after+Responder+XX.pdf)
- Cohen: "a redouble always shows 10+. After our redouble, any subsequent doubles are penalty." — [LarryCo, "Over Opps T/O Double"](https://larryco.com/bridge-articles/over-opps-to-double)
- Walker: the redouble "promises 10+ high-card points", "sets up a penalty situation", and after the opponents bid "any double by you or partner is for penalty". — [Karen Walker, "The Redouble"](https://www.kwbridge.com/rdbl.htm)
- Pavlicek System: redouble = "10+; implies no fit; creates forcing auction." — [rpbridge.net 7g19](https://rpbridge.net/7g19.htm)
- Forum dissent on the XX itself: Zelandakh suggests playing XX artificially (e.g. as a transfer) and notes "a strong trend" toward this among top players. — [BBO forum 58530](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/58530-competitive-bidding-questions/)

**Opener's pass after (bid) is forcing**
- BWS 2017 §V.F(a): "After one of a suit–(double)–redouble–(bid)–?, opener's pass is forcing everywhere." §V.F(b): "After one of a suit–(double)–redouble–(pass)–pass–(bid)–?, responder's pass is forcing everywhere." — [BWS 2017](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Todd: "the general rule is that the opponents may not play an undoubled contract below Opener's suit at the two level." Example 1M X XX 2m P P: "In this auction Opener's pass is forcing. Responder may not pass the auction out. Responder must either double the opponents for penalty or bid a suit". (Suit symbols were lost in PDF extraction. The example is 1-major X XX 2-minor P P.) — [Todd TWiB 145](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5127d3d2e4b0b304f0b6db24/t/58dd43f8c534a52312bb72c5/1490895865693/%28145%29+Bidding+after+Responder+XX.pdf)
- Mackinnon: "The simplest example of a forcing pass is the pass by the opening bidder after a redouble by partner." — [Mackinnon, "The Puppet Pass", 2012](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- raist (BBO), on 1♦-(dbl)-rdbl-(2♣): "passing 2C would be forcing, and thus should promise full opening values at least". — [BBO forum 41472](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/41472-continuations-over-redouble/)
- Vampyr (BBO) reports that forcing status varies by partnership: some say never, some always, some only over 2♣/2♦ but not 2♥/2♠ (fetch-tool summary, not a verbatim quote). — [BBO forum 58530](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/58530-competitive-bidding-questions/)

**Opener's direct rebid = weak / unsuitable for defence**
- Lindop box "OPENER'S REBID AFTER A REDOUBLE": "• Pass, with most hands. • Bid, with a weak, distributional hand. • Double advancer's bid for penalty." Example 1♠ X XX 2♦, holding ♠KQ98752 ♥84 ♦3 ♣KQ4: "2♠. We've opened 1♠ with a weak, distributional hand and don't want to defend for penalty even if partner doubles 2♦. So, we let partner know the nature of our hand right away." — [Lindop 2008](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Cohen: "If the opener runs prematurely, he is showing a weak shapely hand" (example 1♠-(X)-XX-(2♣); 2♠). — [LarryCo](https://larryco.com/bridge-articles/over-opps-to-double)
- BWS 2017 §V.F(d): "A nonjump, nonreverse new-suit bid by opener is nonforcing." — [BWS 2017](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Mackinnon: "If opener bids a suit immediately it is non-forcing, leaving further action to the redoubler." "In Case IV opener may have to bid 2♦ immediately to show weakness." — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- raist: "the proper way to show weakness is to rebid 2D immediately over 2C". bluecalm: "Standard is that bidding directly over rdbl is the weakest action." Dissent: kenberg relays Reese, "According to my old-fashioned notions the way to show weakness is to pass" (second-hand; Reese's original text not located). — [BBO forum 41472](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/41472-continuations-over-redouble/)
- ggwhiz: "if opener bids in front of the redoubler (say 1nt or 1 of a major) it shows a shapely or balanced dog". — [BBO forum 63561](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/63561-simple-redoubled-auction/)

**Opener's double**
- Lindop, 1♠ X XX 2♦ holding ♠AK862 ♥82 ♦QJ105 ♣A3: "Double. Here we have an excellent hand if partner is interested in defending. We can let partner know we have length and strength in diamonds. If we pass, partner might have cards in hearts and clubs but not want to defend if the opponents have found a good fit. Partner doesn't have to pass the double with other things in mind." This is penalty with trumps, but partner may still pull. — [Lindop 2008](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- BWS 2017 §II default (a): "when a pass would be forcing, a double discourages further offensive bidding." Default (b), when pass is non-forcing: penalty if the doubler is limited, cooperative-takeout if unlimited (paraphrase by the fetch tool). §V.F(c): responder's redouble followed by a penalty double is "a weaker penalty suggestion" than a pass-then-double (paraphrase plus a short quote). — [BWS 2017](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)
- Kantar, as reported by Mackinnon, on 1♥ X XX 2♣ (P P ?): with Hand A ♠A9 ♥KQ987 ♦Q8 ♣QT76 Kantar doubles (clubs, so a penalty double). With Hand B ♠A87 ♥AKJ85 ♦976 ♣82 Kantar passes. "Kantar suggests that opener pass on any flat hand, weak, strong, or in-between." — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Mackinnon's own alternative: "A direct double is cooperative, saying, 'I don't have a good double of clubs, but you can pass'". He also proposes the "puppet pass": "let a pass ask partner to double unless he has an exceptional hand, say, a limit raise in hearts." — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Forum split: Vampyr: "I believe it is standard among good players to agree that after a redouble, the first double is takeout." Zelandakh: a pass/double inversion (first double takeout) is common among experts. P_Marlowe: the standard is that a first X in a forcing-pass sequence is penalty, though X can be takeout if played consistently (summaries except Vampyr's quote). — [BBO forum 58530](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/58530-competitive-bidding-questions/)

### Inferences
- Polish Club's current rule matches the published consensus on two points: XX starts the penalty process, and opener's doubles are penalty. It also matches on "Bid 2♣ or below: to compete" if "compete" means weak and shapely: Lindop, Cohen, Todd, BWS and Mackinnon all make the direct bid the weakest, non-forcing action.
- Where it departs: no natural-system source labels the forcing pass a "catchall takeout". In Lindop, Kantar and BWS the pass is the default for most hands, including balanced, strong, and two-suited hands that intend to bid later. Under BWS's default the double "discourages further offensive bidding", which is the opposite pole from a takeout-flavoured pass.
- The "pass = takeout, double = penalty" pairing has some expert currency on BBO, but there it appears as the reverse: first double takeout, pass penalty-inviting. Mackinnon's "puppet pass" also makes the pass ask for a double. So the Polish Club pairing (pass = takeout catchall, X = penalty) looks like the classic pass/double reading, not the inverted one. It agrees with Lindop/BWS on the double. It differs from them on the pass only in calling it "takeout".

### Gaps
- SAYC booklet (ACBL), 2/1 notes by Max Hardy, Bridge Guys, the BBO "2/1 GF Advanced"/GIB system notes, and EBU Standard English/Foundation Acol were not fetched within the tool budget. I found no online Acol text addressing this auction; the Acol search returned only BBO forums. Larry Cohen "F or NF part 4" turned up in search but was not read.
- Kantar's own website text (cited by Mackinnon) was not located. Only Mackinnon's report of it is used.
- Reese's "the way to show weakness is to pass" is a second-hand forum relay only.
- The BWS §V.F(c) and §II(b) wordings are fetch-tool paraphrases, and the major-suit XX section number is uncertain (see above).
- ESRA magazine "Redouble Trouble" (Stephen Freeman, 2025) returned HTTP 403: https://magazine.esra.org.il/posts/entry/redouble-trouble.html
- bridgebum.com/redouble.php returned 404. Barbara Seagram's teachbridge.com redouble article redirects to bridge.com (not followed). bridgeworld.com "Responses Over a Takeout Double" (bida013) returned 404.
- No source found distinguishes 1♣ from other one-suit openings for opener's continuations.

## 2. After 1x (X) XX (P): when does opener pass (to play), when does he bid?

### Takeaway
Standard practice (Lindop, Todd, Quested, Kantar via Mackinnon) is that opener passes with almost every hand. If the doubler also passes, 1xXX is left in, because responder's 10+ sitting over the doubler should make it. Opener bids at once only with a weak, distributional hand (non-jump, non-forcing) or, per Todd, jumps to force to game with extra values and shape. Forum experts describe opener's pass here as "no preference, you pick" rather than an explicit offer to play.

### Cited Findings
- Lindop: "Since responder has not yet had a chance to describe the hand other than it contains 10 or more high-card points, opener usually passes, waiting to hear what responder says next. The only exceptions are when opener has a weak, distributional hand, or when advancer bids and opener wants to double to suggest defending for penalty." Example 1♥ X XX P, holding ♠A93 ♥AKJ83 ♦762 ♣J4: "Pass. We have a typical opening. There's no reason to say anything at this point." On the doubler passing too: "we should have little trouble taking at least seven tricks when partner holds 10 or more high-card points, sitting over the high cards in the East hand. Seven tricks in 1♥ redoubled gives us a game bonus". — [Lindop 2008](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Todd: "If RHO passes after partner has made a redouble, then the opening bidder has several bids that show extra values and are clearly forcing – and actually game forcing." "Any jump bid, whether a jump rebid or a jump in a new suit, shows extra values and is game forcing." (A jump rebid shows a 6+ card suit; a jump shift a 5+ card suit.) "Other direct bids that are not jumps show minimum distributional hands, and are non-forcing." (A non-jump rebid shows 6+; a new suit 5+.) "With a less distributional hand, Opener passes Responder's redouble and waits to see what LHO and partner do next. This allows the partnership to double the opponents for penalty when they bid a new suit." — [Todd TWiB 145](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5127d3d2e4b0b304f0b6db24/t/58dd43f8c534a52312bb72c5/1490895865693/%28145%29+Bidding+after+Responder+XX.pdf)
- Quested: "Unless he has a very shapely hand opener nearly always passes." — [Pattaya BC, "After a redouble", 2007](https://www.pattayabridge.com/conventions/after-a%20redouble.doc)
- Pavlicek: "Rebid structure is unchanged unless an opponent bids." Taken literally, this means opener's rebids over (P) keep their uncontested meanings, which contrasts with Todd's jumps-GF / non-jumps-weak scheme. — [rpbridge.net 7g19](https://rpbridge.net/7g19.htm)
- Mycroft (BBO): "if they leave us in 1♦XX, we have enough strength to make it even if it's their suit". The most common meaning for the pass is "I don't have a preference where to play, you pick" (this quote is about the doubler's side passing). — [BBO forum 63561](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/63561-simple-redoubled-auction/)
- Advancer passing XX is normal. Lindop: "After a redouble, advancer is no longer obliged to bid." Advancer should "Bid with a distinct preference for an unbid suit" and "Pass with no real preference." — [Lindop 2008](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)

### Inferences
- In these sources opener never needs to bid over (P) to stop a "convert to play" accident. Passing it round to the doubler is the normal waiting move, and 1xXX passed out is a welcome result. There are two schemes for opener's immediate bids over (P). Todd: non-jumps weak/NF, jumps GF. Pavlicek: "unchanged".
- For a 1♣ opening that may be short (Polish Club), Lindop's "even if it's their suit" logic (Mycroft) is weaker, since 1♣XX may be passed out by a doubler with real clubs. No source addresses short or artificial club openings here.

### Gaps
- No source covers opener's 1NT or reverse over (P) after XX specifically.
- SAYC/ACBL texts not checked.

## 3. Does any source split the forcing pass into "takeout/catchall" vs "willing to defend"?

### Takeaway
No published natural source defines opener's forcing pass as a takeout catchall. The pass is the default and includes hands willing to defend. Lindop and Mackinnon/Kantar instead distinguish the strong, offensively oriented hand by sequence: pass first, then bid ("pass-and-pull"), which shows a sound/strong hand. Only Mackinnon's "puppet pass" and some BBO experts' first-double-takeout inversion change the meanings of the pass or the double.

### Cited Findings
- Lindop, 1♠ X XX 2♦ holding ♠AQJ92 ♥AK863 ♦7 ♣J5: "Pass. We have a good hand and don't intend to defend for penalty if partner doubles. But if we bid now, we show a weak hand. By passing, and then bidding later, we show a sound opening, with the values for at least game opposite responder's 10+ points." — [Lindop 2008](https://www.betterbridge.com/misc/StandardArticles/Standard200809.pdf)
- Mackinnon: "After partner doubles, opener will pass or bid a suit, forcing, to show a good hand unsuitable for a penalty." "We treat 'pass-and-pull' as strong, so we need only to bid 2♦ after the double to force." Hand C ♠A4 ♥AK875 ♦KQT7 ♣97 (1♥ X XX 2♣): both Kantar and Mackinnon pass, then bid 2♦, forcing. — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Kantar, per Mackinnon: "opener pass on any flat hand, weak, strong, or in-between". So the pass is a catch-all for flat hands of any strength, not specifically takeout. — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- Mackinnon's "puppet pass" proposal makes the pass ask partner to double: "let a pass ask partner to double unless he has an exceptional hand". He says Kantar's approach leaves responder unsure whether partner's later double is a command or a suggestion (fetch-tool summary). — [Mackinnon](https://bobmackinnon.bridgeblogging.com/2012/07/16/the-puppet-pass/)
- raist: opener's forcing pass "should promise full opening values at least" (a strength floor, not a shape split). — [BBO forum 41472](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/41472-continuations-over-redouble/)
- BWS 2017: with a forcing pass available, a double "discourages further offensive bidding" (§II(a)). By implication the pass is the more offence-tolerant call. — [BWS 2017](https://www.bridgeworld.com/pages/readingroom/bws/bwscompletesystem.html)

### Inferences
- The BWS/Lindop/Kantar mainstream gives three tiers: direct bid = weak and shapely; double = penalty interest with trumps; pass = everything else, with pass-then-bid = strong/sound. Polish Club's "Forcing pass: catchall takeout" is closest to this "everything else" tier. Labelling it "takeout" is unusual: mainstream sources stress that the pass also covers hands happy to pass partner's penalty double (Lindop's ♠A93 ♥AKJ83 hand: "we should be happy to pass").
- The Polish Club cuebid (GF two-suiter) and "Other: 18+, natural" have a partial analogue: Todd's jumps over (P) are game forcing, and Lindop shows game-going two-suiters by pass-then-bid rather than by an immediate strong bid. No natural source surveyed uses an immediate cuebid or a 3NT 15–17 by opener in this auction.

### Gaps
- No source found ties the forcing-pass definition to a 1♣ (possibly short) opening.
- Expert texts in book form (Robson & Segal, *Partnership Bidding at Bridge*; Crowhurst, *Acol in Competition*), which Mackinnon cites, are not online and were not checked.
