# What opener's calls mean after 1♣ (X) XX

Checked 10 November 2026. The question: after 1♣ (X) XX, what do system
notes, convention cards, expert articles and forums say opener's pass, double,
cheap bids, cuebids, notrump bids and jumps mean, whether the doubler's partner
passes or bids? How do they compare with the book's current list in
[Opener.md][book]? The evidence sections record precedents only; the last
section is analysis. Nothing here edits the book.

The book's rule, as printed under "After XX":

> The key feature of XX is to start penalty process. Our further doubles are
> penalty rather than takeout.
>
> - **Forcing pass:** catchall takeout
> - **Double:** penalty
> - **Bid 2♣ or below:** to compete
> - **Cuebid:** game-forcing two-suited takeout, clubs and a new suit
> - **3NT:** 15--17, to play
> - **Other:** 18+, natural

The book's XX is "PEN, 9+" ([Competition.md][comp]). Its 1♣ is "12–14 with
2–4 cards in each suit", "11–16 with (5+♣ or 4414)" or "17+ unsuitable for
1♦ and 1NT" ([1C.md][1c]).

Labels. A **specific** source names an auction after 1♣ (X) XX (or 1m (X) XX)
and gives opener's meaning. A **generic** source states a blanket rule for
"one of a suit" or for any redouble, which reaches 1♣ only by application;
those entries carry ‡. The 1♣ types are **Polish-type** (multi-way with a weak
balanced range, Swedish-style clubs included), **unbalanced ♦** (1♣ balanced
or clubs, 1♦ unbalanced), **strong** (15+) and **natural** (sources
written for any one-suit opening). Ranges are quoted as printed. This report
builds on [opener-1d-after-redouble.md][prior], which covers opener's 1♦ after
1♣ (X) XX (P) and the 2,310-card sweep; neither is repeated here.

**Conclusion 1.** No source gives a full list of opener's calls after 1♣ (X)
XX. Four sources name the auction and give opener's meaning. Three of them
have a non-strong 1♣: [Krawczyk–Szymański][ks] (Polish-type) print only a
forcing limit and no XX meaning beyond it, [Stancescu–Draghicescu][sd]
(unbalanced ♦) have a penalty-minded XX and give pass and double in a rule
they share with 1♣ (X) P, and [Judy][judy] (natural 1m) proposes a
weak-notrump 1NT without a quoted XX meaning. [Dubois's Precision][ip] names
it with a constructive XX. [Moscito][mos] names 1♣ (X) XX only as a response
step, so its opener's calls come from general rules. Everything else is a
generic rule.

**Conclusion 2.** A forcing pass and penalty doubles after XX are the generic
mainstream, and the book agrees on both. The closest card is
[Gardenghi–Puerto][gp] (unbalanced ♦): "forcing pass until 2NT", "later X for
penalties". The book sets no level where the force ends. The sources range
from "below two of the opened suit" ([Wolff][wolff]; also [Todd][todd] and
[Nilsland][nil1], whose "below 2♥" is "(two of the opening suit)" after 1♥)
through "our double or 1NT or 2♣/♦" (Krawczyk–Szymański) to "until 2NT"
(Gardenghi–Puerto, [Sucharda–Andruszkiewicz][sa]) and "everywhere"
([BWS][bws]).

**Conclusion 3.** No source calls opener's forcing pass takeout. Where it is
described, pass is the default for most hands, including hands happy to
defend, and pass-then-bid is the strong route ([Lindop][lindop1],
[Mackinnon][mack] reporting Kantar, [Crosby][crosby], [Jassem][neill05]).
[Fools][fools] and Lindop/Kantar split pass and double by trump holding in the
book's direction: double with trumps, pass with the rest. Only some BBO posters
reverse it and play the first double as takeout ([BBO 58530][bbo58530];
[BBO 53068][bbo53068], a 1NT-redouble thread), as do Nilsland's "reversed
penalty doubles", takeout doubles after the redouble above a level the
partnership sets.

**Conclusion 4.** "Bid 2♣ or below: to compete" matches the natural sources
only over an escape below 2♣, where opener's direct bid is the weak, shapely,
non-forcing action. Over 1♣ (X) XX (2♦) or higher, their weak direct rebid is
a three-level bid, which the book's list files under "Other: 18+". The line
also matches Jassem's rule that a bid below two of the opened suit shows a
minimum. At exactly 2♣ the WJ texts part: Jassem's English summary
([article 71][j71]) puts it on the "extras" side, and the Polish wording
([WJ05 short][wjs]) leaves it unaddressed.

**Conclusion 5.** The book's strong direct calls have little precedent. No
source defines opener's cuebid, 3NT or "18+, natural" after 1♣ (X) XX. Natural
sources mostly show strength by pass-then-bid and read direct rebids, new
suits or jumps as weak ([Lindop][lindop1], [Timm][timm]). There are two
exceptions. [Todd][todd]'s jumps over (P) are game-forcing. Timm says that
with "a strong two-suited hand that is not suited to a penalty double, opener
must bid", and that "a reverse would show a strong hand". The unbalanced-♦
Fools reads opener's jumps over (P) as "semiPRE". The nearest Polish-type
support is Jassem's "powyżej dwóch w kolor otwarcia – nadwyżki", generic and
only in the WJ05-era texts: in [WJ2000][wj00] a direct three-level bid over a
2♥ escape still shows a minimum. [Sundelin–Sylvan][ss]'s "he shows 17+"
applies over their transfer XX, not a penalty one.

**Conclusion 6.** After (P) the sources split, and the book's list does not
treat (P) separately. Natural sources pass with nearly all hands
([Lindop][lindop1], Todd, [Quested][quested]). Lindop welcomes 1xXX played
("a game bonus"), and Quested warns the doubling side that the redoublers
"will generally make the redoubled contract". Fools pass with "GF any ... or
weak bal", and Judy bids 1NT with the weak notrump. Stancescu–Draghicescu's
"pass showing min bal" may apply too, but their line most likely follows
responder's pass or XX and does not say whether fourth hand passed or bid. No
source tests leaving a possibly short 1♣ redoubled.

**Conclusion 7.** The evidence is thin and correlated. It comes from 24
authorships and 9 BBO threads: 5 Polish-type (Sundelin–Sylvan's with a
transfer XX), 3 unbalanced ♦, 2 strong and 14 natural. The six WJ texts are
one author, and WJ2000 differs from the WJ05-era texts on direct bids above
two of the opened suit. Among non-strong club systems, only
Krawczyk–Szymański and Stancescu–Draghicescu name the auction.

Marks used in the tables:

- ‡ Generic rule, applied to 1♣ (X) XX rather than stated for it.
- § Suit symbols were lost in extraction; how each was handled is noted in the
  row.
- (fetch) The note-taker's fetch tool paraphrased or summarized the text, so
  the wording is not verbatim.
- (low) The source is terse, second-hand, or a forum post, so treat the entry
  as weak.

All six WJ texts are Jassem's, so they count as one source. Their examples use
1♠ with fourth hand bidding. WJ2000 and the WJ05-era texts differ on a direct
bid above two of the opened suit (see the WJ2000 row).

## XX as a transfer

Where XX transfers to diamonds, opener completes it or declines, and the
penalty structure is gone. [The 1♦ report][prior] records
[Sublime][sublime]'s '1♦: 2–3 ♦s, weak hand', '1NT: 2–3 card support, weak NT
with good stop', '2♦: 4+ ♦s, weak hand' and 'others: natural, strong hand', and
the WJ XXI plus adaptation's 'pas otwierającego na rekontrę wskazuje na siłę
15+' (opener's pass of the redouble shows 15+), from a 1♥ example
([bridgeok.cba.pl][wjxxi]).

[Sundelin–Sylvan][ss] (Swedish club, 11–13 balanced or 17+, inferred from the
1♣–1M rebids) treat 1♣ (X) as a club overcall and use a transfer structure:
'Over (1X) being ♦or ♥ and (D=♣) we use a transfer structure. "Accepting"
transfer at 1-level shows 3, else SYSON.' The structure includes 'D =
4+(X+1), 8+hp' and '1♠ = neg D (transfer to 1NT) (=♦ if D=♣)', so their XX
over 1♣ (X) is a transfer-style call. Their general rule, '1.1 1♣ in comp — If
opener acts over interference with anything but a raise or acceptance of an
invitation he shows 17+', therefore applies over a transfer XX, not a penalty
one. No other source in this pass adds an opener rule for a transfer XX over
1♣ (X) ([BBO 85599][bbo85599]).

## Analysis

The book's list has no source to check it against as a whole. Each line is
compared with the nearest generic rule.

| Book | Sources that agree | Sources that differ |
|---|---|---|
| Forcing pass | WJ2000, BWS, Todd, Mackinnon, Timm, Cuppaidge, Gardenghi–Puerto, Krawczyk–Szymański (to a limit) | Nilsland (not forcing over 2♠ after 1♥); BBO force limits around 2♥ |
| … catchall takeout | None labels it takeout. Nearest: Crosby 2005, 'I lack a penalty double', though he still honours partner's double | Lindop, Kantar, Crosby 2003: pass covers hands happy to defend |
| Double: penalty | Lindop, Cohen, Walker, Todd, Cuppaidge, Timm ('4+ cards'), Fools ('4+ trumps'), Gardenghi–Puerto | Stancescu–Draghicescu ('18-20 bal'); Pavlicek and Mackinnon (cooperative); Nilsland's reversed doubles and BBO 'first double is takeout' |
| Bid 2♣ or below: to compete | Over an escape below 2♣: Lindop, Cohen, Todd, BWS, Mackinnon (proposal), Timm, Crosby; WJ below two of the opened suit | Jassem's English at exactly 2♣ ('extras'); over a two-level escape the natural weak rebid is a three-level bid, outside this line |
| Cuebid: GF two-suiter | None | None |
| 3NT: 15–17 | None | — |
| Other: 18+, natural | WJ05-era 'above two ... extras'; Todd's GF jumps over (P); Timm's strong two-suiter and reverse | WJ2000 (direct three-level bid over 2♥ shows a minimum); Timm (rebid weak, new suit weak 5-5); Fools (jumps 'semiPRE'); Lindop, Cohen and Crosby (direct rebid weak, strength through pass-then-bid) |

**The penalty frame is mainstream; the label on the pass is not.** The forcing
pass and the penalty double follow the classical natural default and the one
card that matches the book's structure (Gardenghi–Puerto). Calling the pass
"takeout" is new. In Lindop's scheme, opener's double shows trumps, a direct
bid shows a weak distributional hand, and pass covers the rest ("most
hands"). Kantar, as reported, passes "any flat hand". In practice the book's
pass may play the same way. The difference is what happens when opener passes
and responder doubles. In WJ, Lindop, Crosby and Mackinnon, opener's pull then
shows the strong or shapely hand. The book puts its strong hands on direct
calls, so it leaves pass-then-pull unassigned. A cost that no source states,
this report's own inference: with a pure penalty double, a hand with defence
but without their suit can only pass. Nilsland's stated cost belongs to the
opposite method, takeout doubles after the redouble, which lose the penalty
when both partners hold their suit.

**The force needs a ceiling.** Natural sources that state one put it at two of
the opened suit (Wolff, Todd, Nilsland). BBO posters on 1NT redoubles put it
at two of a minor or 2♥. For a 1♣ opening, "below two of the opened suit"
covers only one-level escapes. Krawczyk–Szymański, the only Polish-type card
that names 1♣, force through 2♦. Gardenghi–Puerto and Sucharda–Andruszkiewicz
force to 2NT. The book's list says only "Forcing pass", which BWS reads as
"everywhere".

**Direct strength is the deliberate departure.** Natural one-suit openings can
afford weak direct bids because their strong hands mostly pass and pull later.
Even so, Timm has opener bid at once with a strong two-suiter, and BWS's
"nonjump, nonreverse new-suit bid by opener is nonforcing" leaves jumps and
reverses outside its weak class. The book's 17+ 1♣ includes strong
major-suit hands that natural systems open 1M, since its 1♥♠ is capped at
"11–16" ([Openings.md][open]). Jassem's WJ05-era "nadwyżki" above two of the
opened suit points the same way as "Other: 18+, natural", but WJ2000's direct
three-level bid over a 2♥ escape shows a minimum, so even Jassem's support is
not settled. Sundelin–Sylvan's 17+ applies over a transfer XX. No source
defines a cuebid or a 3NT. The book's 1NT opening is "15–17" with "2–6♣"
([Openings.md][open]), so a 15–17 1♣ comes from the
5+♣ or 4414 branch (up to 16) or the 17+ branch unsuitable for 1NT. That makes
the 3NT a club-based or shapely hand, which no source describes. The 18+ floor
also sits one point above the 1♣ strong branch's 17+.

**The 2♣ boundary follows the natural sources, not Jassem.** The book counts
2♣ as competitive. That matches Todd's non-jump rebid and Lindop's weak
rebid, and the unbalanced-♦ Fools ('rebid suit : 6+suit, min'). Jassem's
English puts two of the opened suit with extras, and his weak 1♣ variant does
not rebid clubs on the second round, so his line fits a 1♣ whose minimum
hands seldom hold long clubs. The book's 11–16 branch holds 5+♣, so a minimum
2♣ has a natural home there. The match stops at one-level escapes. Over 1♣
(X) XX (2♦) or higher, every direct bid falls under "Other: 18+", while the
natural sources make a direct suit rebid at that level weak (Lindop's 2♠ over
2♦, Cohen's 2♠ over 2♣, Timm's "rebid of the suit"). So the book has no
direct weak call over a two-level escape, and a weak shapely hand must pass.

**(P) is a different auction, and the list does not split it out.** After
1♣ (X) XX (P), opener's pass is not forcing in the usual sense. It lets the
doubler leave 1♣XX in. There is no contract opener can double, and the doubler
has shown no suit to cuebid. That leaves the cheap bids, 3NT and "Other". The
book's weak 1♣ may hold two clubs. Mycroft's strength argument on BBO, that
1♦XX makes "even if it's their suit", would hold for a short club too; the
doubt is this report's own, and no source tests it. Fools passes with a weak
balanced hand anyway, and so do Stancescu–Draghicescu if their line covers
(P); Judy bids 1NT with it. The earlier table's "P = S/O, 4+♣" and "1NT =
BAL MIN" (recorded in [the 1♦ report][prior]) made this choice; the current
list leaves it open. No source simulates or tests a
short 1♣ redoubled, so whether pass needs club length is a judgment or
simulation question this survey cannot answer.
