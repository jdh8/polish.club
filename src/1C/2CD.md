# Swapped 2♣♦

The positive responses 2♣♦ show 5+ cards in the other minor suit.  They are
game-forcing by an unpassed hand, like 2/1.  This method helps find minor suit
slams.  I spell out the minor suits because there is a subtle difference between
them.  Finding a big fit in clubs is easier because opener can have a minimum
hand with long clubs.

Opener reverses with extra values to utilize higher steps to send information.
This avoids an awkward situation Bridge World calls "two temporizers and zero
describers."  Deviating from *Polish Club 2020: Expert*, I define 1♣ - 2♣♦ - 2♥♠
as reverses.  I believe this treatment helps find the best contract.  When we
only have a game, we want to play in a major suit despite a better minor fit.
However, we play the best fit in a slam to maximize our chances, especially at
IMPs.

Over the 2♣ response that shows diamonds, jumps to 3♦♥♠ are natural because
there is no assured fit yet.  Moreover, 3♥♠ usually indicate diamond shortage
and suggest major suit games instead.

| 1♣ - 2♣ - | |
|-----|-|
| 2♦  | NAT, 11--14 or 18+, 3+♦
| 2♥  | S/T, 17+, 5+♥
| 2♠  | S/T, 17+, 5+♠
| 2NT | NAT, 11--14 or 18+, 0--2♦
| 3♣  | NAT, 11--14 or 18+, 6+♣
| 3♦  | S/T, 15+, 3+♦
| 3♥♠ | S/T, 15+, 4+#, 5+♣
| 3NT | COG, 15--17, 0--2♦

Over the 2♦ response that shows clubs, jumps to 3♦♥♠ are Splinter bids because a
fit in clubs is already established.

| 1♣ - 2♦ - | |
|-------|-|
| 2♥    | S/T, 17+, 5+♥
| 2♠    | S/T, 17+, 5+♠
| 2NT   | NAT, 12--14 or 18+
| 3♣    | NAT, 11--14 or 18+, 4+♣
| 3♦♥♠! | SPL, 15+, 0--1#, 4+♣
| 3NT!  | COG, 15--17, 4+♣

1♣ - 2♣♦ - 3NT handles 15--17 choice of games because these hands would be
otherwise hard to describe.  It is usually fine to group 11--14 and 18+ together
because the latter can unilaterally explore slams, given a partner already
game-forcing.
