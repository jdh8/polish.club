# Completing a competitive transfer with a doubleton

Checked 4 November 2026. The question: after 1♣ (X) 1♦/1♥ or 1♣ (1♦/1♥) X,
where responder's call is a transfer to a major and fourth hand passes, may
opener complete the transfer with a minimum balanced hand and only two cards
in the major? Or does completion promise three, with the balanced doubleton
rebidding 1NT? The evidence sections record precedents only; the last section
is analysis. Nothing here edits the book.

Auction labels used below: **(a)** 1♣ (X) 1♦/1♥; **(b)** 1♣ (1♦) X = ♥;
**(c)** 1♣ (1♥) X = ♠. “Inherited” means the source states only an
uncontested completion rule plus “system on” in competition. It never says
what completion shows after the interference. Ranges are quoted as printed.
Non-English text is quoted verbatim, with our translation in brackets. Suit
symbols that were dropped from a PDF's text layer have been restored from the
rendered page or from Symbol-font codepoints.

The uncontested question (two-card completion after 1♣–1♦/1♥) is covered in
[t-walsh-sources.md][shortlist], and Cachalot in
[t-walsh-competition-free-bid.md][cachalot]. Both are summarized here, not
repeated.

[shortlist]: https://github.com/jdh8/muscat-walsh/blob/main/research/t-walsh-sources.md
[cachalot]: https://github.com/jdh8/muscat-walsh/blob/main/research/t-walsh-competition-free-bid.md

**Conclusion 1.** Over a takeout double (a), the sources that write a rule
for this auction split. Todd, Clayton, Beall, rod and van der Meulen make
completion exactly three, and agum… three (never two), with 1NT for the
balanced hand without the fit. Fallenius–Welland and Ash–Ferrari
(partnership notes) and Ravera (forum) keep completion as uncontested and so
allow two; DavidKok (forum, 2023) allows it by extending his overcall rule.
Both sides mostly carry over an uncontested rule. Only Todd, Clayton and
agum… give reasons tied to the double.

**Conclusion 2.** Tournament convention cards lean towards two, but only by
inheritance. About fourteen cards (mostly British and Dutch) define
uncontested completion as 2–3 and say “system on” over the double. About nine
inherit an exactly-3 rule the same way. None of these cards states a separate
competitive completion rule. Of the 2–3 cards that print a 1NT rebid range,
all use 1NT for a strong (17+ or 18–20) balanced hand, so for them completion
is the *only* weak balanced rebid.

**Conclusion 3.** Over an overcall (b, c), the French Cachalot sources, Beall
and rod make completion exactly three, and Cachalot's 1NT usually shows a
stopper. The stopperless doubleton is filled three ways: Fauché–Mazères let
1NT drop the stopper, Ash–Ferrari complete in (c) with “a hand unable to
rebid 1NT”, and DavidKok completes with any balanced minimum.

**Conclusion 4.** When fourth hand doubles the transfer, every source agrees:
completion = exactly three, and pass = a balanced hand with one or two. The
double gives the doubleton hand a free call, and when it does, nobody spends
completion on the doubleton.

DavidKok (BBO Forums, 16 Aug 2023), writing for a strong or Dutch Doubleton
1♣, gives 1♣ (X) “1 ♦ = SP+ 4(+) ♥ , Walsh style” but states his completion
rule only for overcalls (see that section). Applying it to (a) is our
extension.

**Neither side owns the argument from the double.** Beall (“just as if the
double had not occurred”), rod (“All this is on if second hand doubles…”)
and van der Meulen (“blijven … hetzelfde”) carry their exactly-3 rule over
from the uncontested structure, just as Fallenius–Welland and Ash–Ferrari
carry over theirs. Only Todd, Clayton and agum… argue from the double itself.
For Fallenius–Welland and Ash–Ferrari, 1NT is not available for a minimum
(17–19, 18–19); Ravera does not give a range.

Same-thread rebuttal: in reply to Hargreaves's claim (below) that completion
shows three, Florian Alter asks: “Does opener always show a 3-card suit? If
so, that has the disadvantage of revealing unnecessary information to the
opponents and sometimes it might wrongside a NT contract.”
([comment 496287](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/transfers-over-a-takeout-double/?cj=496287)).
With DavidKok's rule, it is one of two explicit arguments against exactly
three.

**Where a 2–3 card prints a 1NT rebid range, it is strong.**
Adamson–Valentine–Glen (18–20), Deschepper–Plackett (17–19),
Selway–Preddy (18–19), Molenaar–Verbeek (18–20), Valentine–O'Brien (17+–19)
and Hargreaves–McOrmond (17–19) leave completion as the only call for the
weak balanced hand. The other 2–3 cards print no range. The 3-card cards keep
1NT for that hand (Cunningham–Cunningham: “1NT with 2/1 and 11/13 hcp”). So
the split follows where the 1NT rebid sits. One player shows this directly:
Sophie Cunningham's 2025 cards with Cheung and with Paul Cunningham say “3”,
while her 2026 card with Pierce says “2/3 cards 11-13” and “1N 17-19”.

**Silent cards (transfers on over X, completion undefined).**
Gawryś–Klukowski (Polish Club, Yeh Bros Cup 2019) play natural uncontested

**The stopper is the problem specific to overcalls.** With exactly-3
completion and a 1NT that shows a stopper, a minimum balanced hand with two
cards and no stopper has no call. Cazaux and Beall leave this hand
undescribed. Fauché–Mazères (Cachalot, 2011) bid 1NT: “l'ouvreur peut être
2344 ou 2353 et dire 1SA sans arrêt Coeur” [opener may be 2344 or 2353 and
bid 1NT without a heart stopper]
([PDF p. 4](http://dsbridge.fr/documents/technique/Convention%20Cachalot.pdf#page=4)).
Ash–Ferrari fill the gap by completing (“a hand unable to rebid 1NT”).
Tim Verbeek's IMP column, like Fauché–Mazères, drops the stopper from opener's
1NT after partner's double: “Het idee dat je 1SA kunt bieden zonder stop, is
gemeengoed geworden tegenwoordig.” [The idea that you may bid 1NT without a
stopper has become common property nowadays.] His example is a negative
double, 1♣-(1♠)-dbl-(pas)-1SA, but the same column endorses transfers after
1♣-(1♦)/(1♥) (“In de top spelen we allemaal transfers als er gevolgd wordt
over een lage-kleur-opening” [at the top we all play transfers after an
overcall of a minor opening]) and states the rule by seat (“Zit je ervoor
dan bied je je verdeling en niet je stop” [if you sit in front of the
overcaller, bid your shape, not your stopper]), so it covers (b) and (c). He
does not say what completion shows. He is the Verbeek of Molenaar–Verbeek
([IMP 2021/4, p. 26](https://www.imp-bridge.nl/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/imp4-2021-Tims-Standaard-3.pdf#page=1)).

## Analogues

**Support doubles (natural responses).** The mainstream convention makes the
one-level support action exactly three, and other calls deny three:

- Larry Cohen: “The double says: “Opener has exactly 3 hearts.” Period.”
  ([larryco.com PDF, p. 1](https://www.larryco.com/uploaded/pdf/pdfup_25.pdf#page=1)).
  With a doubleton and no stopper (hand F, 1♦ P 1♥ 1♠): “You don't have
  3-card support (you should pass).” With three hearts and a solid spade
  stopper (hand H), 1NT “would be a much better description”
  ([pp. 3–4](https://www.larryco.com/uploaded/pdf/pdfup_25.pdf#page=3)).
- BridgeBum ranks the support double above showing a stopper: “This is more
  important than rebidding no-trumps to show an enemy stopper” and “the value
  of differentiating 3-card and 4-card support can be a game swing”
  ([bridgebum](https://www.bridgebum.com/support_double.php)).
- Andrew Gumperz: “Bids other than support double should not absolutely deny
  3-card support, although they usually will.” In 1♣ X 1♥ 1♠ with ♠KQ10x
  ♥Jxx he bids 1NT, and “when opener is minimum and 4-3-3-3 … it is
  sometimes better to pass”
  ([Bridge Winners](http://bridgewinners.com/article/view/support-doubles-2/)).
- Haig Tchamitch: “I recommend that you not make a support double with a very
  minimum balanced hand”, and the convention applies whether LHO “passed,
  bid, or doubled”
  ([PDF](https://bridgewebs.com/duplicatelite/Support%20Doubles%20by%20Haig.pdf#page=1)).
- Stephen Tu (BBO, 2018), on how support-double players such as Kokish and
  Woolsey use pass: “pass = deny 3 cd support (strong NT with 2 cds in
  partner's major OR unbalanced hand …)”. Tramticket notes that Rodwell's own
  three examples are all 12–14 balanced
  ([topic 79291](https://bridgebase.com/forums/topic/79291-no-trump-response/page__view__new)).
- Robson & Segal tie the convention to notrump range: “It is probably wrong
  to play support doubles if your play a weak no-trump”, but with a strong
  notrump “you will very often have a minimum balanced hand to open 1c or
  1d. Now support doubles are very useful”
  ([PDF p. 44](https://bridgewithdan.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Robson-Segal-Partnership-Bidding.pdf#page=44)).
- Counter-view, the Granovetters: raising with three “takes up their valuable
  bidding space”, and support doubles cost the penalty double
  ([CSB News reprint](https://csbnews.org/?p=87931)).
- Polish standard: *Wspólny Język* 2005 has no transfers after 1♣ (X), but
  its “Kontra fit przyrzeka trzy karty w kolorze odpowiedzi” [the fit double
  promises three cards in responder's suit]; but after a 1♣ opening the
  double is ambiguous: “kontra fit lub silniejszy wariant trefla” [fit double
  or the strong club]. WJ05 has no support redouble; its redouble is 10+
  ([PDF p. 13](https://stara.pzbs.pl/ksiazki/inni_wydawcy/WJ05StrPol.pdf#page=13)).
  Polish cards reportedly repeat the exactly-3 fit double (Cios–Lichawski,
  Wójcicki–Sielicki, Kowalski–Tuszyński; quotes not linked, unverified).
- Robots: Wbridge5's natural 1♣ (1♦) X – 1NT is “12--14 HCP, BAL, 2--3♥”,
  i.e. two and three together
  ([wbridge5.book](https://github.com/jdh8/wbridge5.book/blob/master/src/1CD/Competition.md#L174-L193)),
  while its support double shows “exactly 3 cards”
  ([support doubles](https://github.com/jdh8/wbridge5.book/blob/master/src/1CD/Competition.md#L360-L364)).
  GIB “may do support X with Kx”
  ([GIB notes](https://www.bridgebase.com/doc/gib_system_notes.php)).

**Two-level transfers allow two.** When completion is at the two level and
there is no cheap notrump, doubleton acceptance is normal: Kirr–Carruthers,
1♦ (2♣) 2♦/2♥, “Opener is expected to accept the transfer with 11-13
balanced and 2/3 of the major … This allows Responder to transfer, expecting
to play in 2M”
([PDF p. 5](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2025EFOTSC/Systems/CANADIANS-CARRUTHERS-KIRR.pdf#page=5));
Adam Meyerson, strong 1♣ (2m), “2M acceptance = NF, doubleton or three-card
support in a min balanced hand”, where the transfer promises five
([topic 66467](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/66467-1c-2m-interference));
Grinberg–Kalish's switch over 1♠/2♣, “2♥ = 2 or 3 cards in ♥ Minimum”
([PDF p. 11](https://r.pzbs.pl/2022/tsc/web/CC/CC%20ISR%20Grinberg-Kalish.pdf#page=11));
and Clayton, quoted above. The doubleton completion appears where it has no
alternative or where responder promises five.

**Uncontested field, for context.** In sfi's count of 2023 Bermuda Bowl
cards, “8 9 pairs played that accepting the transfer shows 3 cards”, “6 pairs
accepted the transfer with balanced minimums”, “1 (NZ) pair played both”, and
in those 2+ cases “A 1NT rebid showed a stronger hand than a 1NT opening”

## Evidence limits

- **No convention card states a competitive completion rule for the
  one-level transfer.** Every card entry above is an uncontested rule plus
  “system on”. The authors may play it differently at the table.
- The explicit exactly-3 sources are teaching material (Todd, Beall), forum
  posts (Clayton, rod, agum…) and a 1998 proposal (van der Meulen). The
  two-card side is two partnership notes (Fallenius–Welland, Ash–Ferrari)
  and two forum posts (Ravera, DavidKok). Only one top-level partnership's
  own notes (Fallenius–Welland) state an (a) rule, and it is “as without
  interference”.
- The leading Polish Club pair, Gawryś–Klukowski, plays transfer doubles and
  support doubles but prints no completion rule.
- No frequency or simulation evidence compares 1M on a 5-2 with 1NT, and
  nothing measures how often a double of a Polish 1♣ holds both majors.
- Ash–Ferrari are internally inconsistent in (c): “SYSTEM ON if 4th hand
  passes”, yet 1NT is “11-13 as system OFF”. Walsh–Goodman's card says both
  “System on” and “Bids natural” over the double.

### Arguments each side gives

- **For exactly three:** LAW, 3 at the one level and 4 at the two level
  (Todd); “like a support double” (Todd; Beall pairs it with support
  doubles), so “responder can make an informed decision” (Hargreaves); the
  double makes it likelier that responder has only four and that trumps
  break badly (Hargreaves); opener declares notrump with the doubler on lead
  (Clayton and Bowles argue this for a transfer to 1NT; it carries over to
  opener's 1NT rebid); 5-3 fits are found easily (rod).
- **For allowing two:** keep one structure, “Double therefore says they took
  my bid” (Fallenius–Welland); accept wherever you would pass a natural
  non-forcing bid (Ravera, DavidKok); stay low on misfits (DavidKok); a 5-2
  at the one level beats 1NT opposite a weak responder (Schendel); 1NT stays
  free for the strong balanced hand (mikeh; every 2–3 card that prints a
  range). Exactly three also gives information away and may wrong-side
  notrump (Alter).

### What distinguishes 1♣ (X) in Polish Club

1. **1NT needs no stopper.** The double named no suit, so opener's 1NT is a
   natural home for 12–14 balanced with 0–2 cards. This is the call that the
   2–3 camp lacks because their 1NT is 17+. Polish Club's uncontested 1NT
   rebid is already 12–14, so the structural reason behind the 2–3 cards
   does not apply here. Over an overcall, the stopper problem returns.
2. **Responder may hold 5 HCP.** After 611d970 the transfers are “5+”, one
   point below Muscat's 6+. A weak responder with four cards opposite a
   two-card completion either passes into a 4-2 fit or rebids 1NT. Beall,
   describing the same rebid opposite his own exactly-3 completion, calls it
   “likely wrong-sided”: responder declares, and the doubler is not on lead.
   With exactly three, opener's 1NT puts the doubler on lead (Bowles, about
   responder's transfer to 1NT: “the lead comes from the strong hand, into
   our strong hand”
   ([cj=496264](https://bridgewinners.com/article/view/transfers-over-a-takeout-double/?cj=496264))).
3. **The doubler is likely to hold the majors** (our reasoning, unsourced).
   A takeout double of 1♣ usually shows major length, so the cards missing
   from a 4-2 fit tend to sit with the doubler. When advancer competes, it
   will often be in a suit where opener has length, so responder faces a
   decision at the two level. Knowing 3 versus 2 is what Hargreaves and Todd
   cite for those decisions. With exactly three, responder with five knows
   of an 8-card fit immediately.
4. **Support-double tradition.** Polish standard already uses the fit double
   (ambiguous after 1♣ in WJ05) and, on some cards, a support redouble for
   exactly three (WJ05, Cios–Lichawski, Wójcicki–Sielicki,
   Kowalski–Tuszyński; the cards unverified). Gawryś–Klukowski play support
   doubles and transfer doubles but leave completion undefined.
   Competition.md itself sells completion as a “support double”.
   Robson–Segal tie support doubles to a strong notrump, and Polish Club's
   1NT is strong, so balanced minimums open 1♣.
5. **Cost.** The 5-2 at the one level is lost; a weak responder with five
   cards opposite 1NT plays 1NT or rebids his suit at the two level. The
   unbalanced 11–17 hand with three cards and the balanced 12–14 hand with
   three both complete, so completion is wide in strength but not in length.

### Lean

On this analysis, **over (X), completion should show exactly three, and 1NT
should be 12–14 balanced with 0–2 cards, without a stopper requirement.**
The explicit sources split. The exactly-three side is mostly teaching
material and forum posts; the two-card side includes the one world-class
partnership's notes. Both sides mostly carry over an uncontested rule. The
deciding argument is structural and ours: the 2–3 sources pair two-card
completion with a strong 1NT rebid, while Polish Club's 1NT rebid is 12–14
and needs no stopper after (X), so the doubleton hand already has a natural
call. This matches Competition.md as it stands. Opener.md's “See my Muscat

**Over an overcall the evidence does not settle it.** The documented choices
are:

- Cachalot: exactly three, 1NT with a stopper. Most sources leave the
  stopperless doubleton undefined; Fauché–Mazères let 1NT drop the stopper.
- Ash–Ferrari in (c): completion = three, **or** a hand that cannot bid 1NT.
  This fills the hole at the price of an occasional doubleton completion.
  The French ibridge fiche and Cazaux (“l'absence d'arrêt dans la couleur
  d'intervention”, p. 1) already make completion deny a stopper.
- DavidKok: balanced minima complete; 1NT shows length in their suit.
- Verbeek, Fauché–Mazères: opener's 1NT after partner's double need not show
  a stopper.

Of these, Ash–Ferrari's wording fixes the gap in the current text with the
smallest change; Fauché–Mazères and Verbeek fix it while keeping completion
at exactly three. That is a design choice for the author, not something the
sources settle. Whichever is chosen, the doubled-transfer case (fourth hand
doubles responder's call) has unanimous support for completion = three and
pass = one or two. The book does not yet define it.
