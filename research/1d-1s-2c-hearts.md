# 1♦ (1♠) 2♣ showing hearts

Checked 17 November 2026. Question: does anyone play this exact auction?

**Yes.** It is an application of **Switch**, exchanging the meanings of the
unbid suits. Two named partnerships document it: **Björn Fallenius–Mats
Nilsland** and **Frances Hinden–Graham Osborne**. The first pair explicitly
prints both directions; the second defines the same mapping in its
supplementary notes. The sources establish published agreements, not how
frequently the auction has occurred in play.

## Primary evidence

is dated January 2025, but interior sections retain older dates. Note 5 also
specifies four hearts for 1m (1♠) X. Neither cited card supplies a complete

that it must be 10+, game-forcing, or a mandatory transfer from the label.

The Swedish federation also hosts [another Fallenius–Nilsland card](https://www.svenskbridge.se/sites/default/files/field_sb_upload/sweseniorsfalleniusnilsland.pdf#page=1)

## Name and history

Brad Coles describes **Switch** in [“The modern transfer,” *Australian
Bridge*, April–May 2008, printed p. 21 / PDF p. 6](https://www.australianbridge.com/article_39-1_transfers.pdf#page=6).
He attributes it to Marty Bergen, citing Matthew Granovetter's *Bridge
Additions 96*. His rule covers either minor opening followed by a 1♠
overcall: swap the unbid suits and retain negative doubles. His stated
benefit is putting the overcaller on opening lead when opener declares.
This is publisher-hosted explanatory material; the Bergen attribution is
second-hand, since the cited book was not inspected.

## Implications for this repository

This section is analysis of [the current 1♦ competitive table](../src/1D/Competition.md),
not a claim about those partnerships' full systems.

The current structure has natural forcing 2♣, heart-showing 2♦, and natural
non-forcing 2♥. Adopting the simple Switch mapping would change that to:

| Response after 1♦ (1♠) | Simple Switch mapping |
| --- | --- |
| 2♣ | Hearts |
| 2♦ | Available for a natural diamond raise |
| 2♥ | Clubs |

Moving hearts down to 2♣ leaves opener a 2♦ rebid below 2♥ and frees
responder's immediate 2♦ for diamond support. The price is moving clubs
up to 2♥ and losing the direct natural non-forcing 2♥ response. The heart
strength bands, negative double, opener's 2♦ rebid, and later competitive
continuations would need explicit agreements. The evidence answers the
precedent question; it does not establish which complete structure is best
for this Polish Club system.

## Follow-up: is 1m (X) 2om consistently hearts too?

**The cited pairs do not document that uniform extension.** A takeout
double leaves all three other suits unbid; Switch's two-suit exchange
after a spade overcall does not automatically apply.

- **Fallenius–Nilsland:** the [Wuhan card's minor-opening competitive columns](https://www.svenskbridge.se/sites/default/files/field_sb_upload/falleniusnilsland.pdf#page=2)
  specify fit jumps after a double. Applying that explicit general rule,
  **1♣ (X) 2♦ is a fit jump**, not a pure heart transfer. Their transfers
  after a double are specified at the one level. **1♦ (X) 2♣ is not a
  jump**, and its exact meaning is not separately stated in this card.
- **Hinden–Osborne:** their [2025 card](https://championships.eurobridge.org/2025CC/Systems/OPEN/O-ENGLAND-HINDEN-OSBORNE.pdf)
  specifies redouble/one-level transfers after 1m (X), while Switch is
  explicitly scoped to spade overcalls. This does not establish the
  proposed two-level extension. Their [county-hosted EBU-format card,
  p. 1](https://www.bridgewebs.com/surrey/TC%20ebu20bFrances%20Hinden%20Graham%20Osborne.pdf#page=1)
  explicitly makes two-level new-suit jumps after a double of 1♣ weak;
  its opening table describes those jumps as natural, 4–8 with six cards.
  Applying these rules gives **1♣ (X) 2♦ = weak diamonds**, not hearts.
  The 2025 card likewise exempts these jumps from its fit-jump rule.
  **1♦ (X) 2♣ remains unverified.**

The distinction between 1♣ (X) 2♦ (a jump) and 1♦ (X) 2♣ (a nonjump)
is essential; shared notation `2om` hides that difference. A partnership
could choose a common heart-showing meaning, but the named precedents
above do not demonstrate it.
