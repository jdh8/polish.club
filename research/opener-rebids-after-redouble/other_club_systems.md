# Opener's continuations after 1♣ (X) XX in non-Polish artificial / nebulous club systems

Scope: strong-club (Precision, Moscito, Blue Club, relay clubs), nebulous/short clubs (Swedish, Spanish/Italian-style 2+♣ with transfer responses, AUC), and transfer-response clubs. Polish/WJ and plain natural systems are excluded. All quotes are as printed. Some PDFs use a font where `{ } [ ]` stand for ♣ ♦ ♥ ♠; that mapping was checked against Moscito's opening table ("1{= 15+H", "1}= 4+[" = 1♦ shows 4+♥, "1]= 6}" = 1♠ shows 6♦, which are Moscito's transfer openings).

Overall finding: no source in this sample spells out a full table of opener's rebids after 1♣ (X) XX the way the Strawberry book does. When 1♣ is strong, XX is almost never "penalty, start the penalty process". It is a re-used response step: the GF positive in Moscito, a 6–8 or 4–6 semi-positive in Precision and Blue Club, or a heart/diamond transfer. Opener then carries on with the uncontested system, and the doubles are takeout, or penalty once game is forced. The forcing-pass, penalty-double structure turns up only in a top-level WBF card with a nebulous 2+♣ (Gardenghi–Puerto), stated as a general rule over any takeout double.

## Q1. When 1♣ is strong (15+/16+/17+), what does XX mean, and how does opener act after 1♣ (X) XX?

### Takeaway
In strong-club systems, XX over a double of 1♣ is a constructive response (positive or semi-positive), not a "let's penalise" call. Opener's later doubles are takeout unless a game force is already in place. Moscito is the clearest case: XX is the GF positive, relays carry on through a further double, and opener's double is "P" (penalty) only because the auction is GF. No strong-club source makes opener's pass forcing over a run-out because of XX.

### Cited Findings

**Moscito 2005 (Paul Marston; PDF metadata: subject "Moscito – The system with a bite", 2005).** 1♣ = "1{= 15+H" (1st/2nd seat); "3rd & 4th 1{= 17+". Uncontested responses: "1} = 6+ QP" (the positive), "1[ = SP" (semi-positive), "1]= junk - 0-4 H". — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- 1♣ (X): "7.1 WE OPEN 1{ — 1. They dbl: pass=0-4, xx=1}, 1}=1[, 1[=1N, 1]=2{ etc." So XX takes over the 1♦ GF positive and every other response moves down one step. — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- General rule for redoubles: "General: Ignore doubles out of relay (xcpt dbl of 1{). Dbl in relay: rdb is TP (no matter how silly), pass is S1 etc. Rdbl non relay is pen suggestion (xcpt 1{, x, xx). Nat conts." The 1♣ (X) XX auction is named as the exception to "redouble = penalty suggestion". — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- After a semi-positive and then a double: "7.2 We open 1{ and give SP, they x, xx is Penalty interest, else as before." — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- Game-force status: "GF exists after 1{:1} or after other openings when R relays…" — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- Opener (relayer) after interference: "7.7 WE RELAY – THEY BID 1. If they dbl, pass by R is relay; pass by RR is S1, bringing the level -1. xx either side is TP. … 2. If they call rather than dbl, relay is off. Then a new suit by RR creates GF, if not already in place. 3. Dbl is P in GF, T/O if not GF." — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- A shorter Moscito write-up (Martin Johnson; "The version described here was developed by professor Bo-Yin Yang of Taiwan") has a notation key ("X Double. xx Redouble.") but nothing on 1♣ (X). — [Moscito system by Martin Johnson](https://www.bridgewebs.com/ocala/moscito.pdf)

**Imprecise Precision, Release 7.1 (Paul F. Dubois).** Strong Precision 1♣. "5.1.1 Responses Over 2nd Seat's Double. If they double after 1♣!, we split the negative bid into two ranges." — [Imprecise Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/ImprecisePrecision.pdf)
- "Pass with 0-5 HCP. Assuming RHO bids, opener behaves as if they opened. Double is takeout, a cue bid is Michaels. A jump to 2N is Unusual." — [Imprecise Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/ImprecisePrecision.pdf)
- "Redouble with 6-8 HCP or with a positive hand with no good bid. Doubles of their suit bid is takeout. Responder promises to bid once more." This is the only explicit opener-side rule after 1♣ (X) XX found for Precision: opener's doubles are takeout, and responder's promise to bid again is what keeps the auction alive (not a forcing pass). — [Imprecise Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/ImprecisePrecision.pdf)
- "Suit bids are positive, game forcing, and show a five-card suit." "1N is a positive response with 8+ HCP and a stopper. It is game forcing." — [Imprecise Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/ImprecisePrecision.pdf)
- Similar case of RHO interfering after 1♣–1♦: "Opener passes with any balanced minimum. • Double (or redouble) by opener is for takeout." — [Imprecise Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/ImprecisePrecision.pdf)

**Starting Precision, Release 2.0 (Dubois).** "If they interfere after 1♣!, we split the negative bid into two ranges. For hands up to 8 HCP: • Pass with 0-4 HCP. • Double (or redouble) with 5-8 HCP or with a minimum positive hand with no good bid." "As the auction proceeds, a double by opener is for takeout." — [Starting Precision](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Starting-Precision.pdf)

**Official ACBL Encyclopedia of Bridge, 7th ed. (2011), "Precision" (quoted on the TCG Defines It site).** "If 1♣ is doubled, normal responses are used, except that with a weak hand responder passes with clubs, bids 1♦ without clubs or redoubles with both major suits." The page has nothing on opener's rebids after XX. — [Encyclopedia of Bridge – Precision](https://sites.google.com/site/tcgdefinesit/home/precision/encyclopedia-of-bridge-precision)

**Precision Simplified for 2/1 GF Players, 2nd ed. (Neil H. Timm, 2011).** Over a meaningless double of 1♣*: "Pass 0-4 HCP / 1♦ 5-7 / You should always ignore the double and just make your standard bids". Over a double showing the majors (vs Mathe): "Pass 0-4 HCP / 1♦ 5-7 / Redouble 8+ / …". The book gives no opener continuation after XX. — [Precision Simplified (Timm)](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Precision-Simplified-For-2-1-Game-Force-Players.pdf)

**Blue Club (sheet on file at CBF Bridge Week 2025, pair A12).** "1♣ opening promises 17 or more HCP or 8 quick tricks". After "1♣ Double x": "Pass shows 0-3 points / Redouble shows 4-6 points (possible values in Clubs) / 1 Diamond shows 4-6 points / All responses with 1 Heart and above remain the same". Nothing on opener's rebid after XX. — [Blue Club bidding (CBF A12)](https://cbf.ca/BWeek/25files/CCards/A12_BlueClubBidding-noMulti-by-opening.pdf)

**SCRAPE / SPREAD relay strong club (ACT team, ABF ANC 2002 system notes).** "Interference after 1C at the one-level - Style One. This interference is insufficient to raise the level of relays damagingly, so pass is full negative, double is semi-positive and bids are full positive. … After the semi-positive double, opener may then game-force relay with Step 1 if the direct-seat opponent passes or redoubles (i.e. the next suit or pass, respectively, is for relay)." "Once relays have begun, relayer's double and redouble in both direct and pass-out seats is for penalty." "If the interference raises the steps more than three steps, then both opener and responder may double for penalty." The notes do not say what responder's XX is over a *double* of 1♣. — [ACT system notes (SCRAPE/SPREAD)](https://abfevents.com.au/events/ANC/2002/systems/actynotes.pdf)

**Symmetric Relay strong club (Lysandra Zheng, notes dated 4 Dec 2024).** "1♣ 15+ HCP, any shape". Relays can still be set up after "1♣-(Dbl)". "If a relay has already been established, it is continued so long as the relay responses are no more than 2 steps up from the original … Pass becomes a default relay bid, and Dbl represents the lowest step." The notes give no specific meaning for XX over 1♣ (Dbl). — [Symmetric Notes](https://nzbridge.co.nz/user/inline/5776/Symmetric_Notes.pdf)

**Meckwell Lite (Zaremba Precision table).** Search snippet only: after 1♣ is doubled, "pass for 0-4 HCP, 1♦ for 5-7 HCP, a double [sic] for 5-7 HCP without a five-card suit, and XX for 8+ HCP with a 4-4 major". The PDF did not download, so this is unverified. — [precision_table.pdf](https://www.zaremba.ch/files/precision_table.pdf) (not fetched)

### Inferences
- Moscito: after 1♣ (X) XX (P), opener relays as in the uncontested 1♣–1♦ auction. The level is one step lower, because the 1♦ step is still free. This is inferred from "pass by R is relay" and "xx=1}". It is not stated for this exact auction.
- Moscito: after 1♣ (X) XX (bid), "relay is off" but GF "exists after 1{:1}", so by rule 7.7.3 opener's double is penalty and his suit bids are natural and game-forcing. This is the closest strong-club parallel to the Strawberry "double = penalty". But it comes from the GF, not from a penalty-oriented XX, and there is no forcing pass at catch-all strength.
- Precision (Dubois): XX is a weak-ish semi-positive (6–8), so opener stays in "takeout" mode. The opposite of the Strawberry penalty structure, but consistent with XX not showing defensive values.
- In none of these systems does XX show interest in penalising the doubler, except Moscito's SP-then-XX (7.2), which is a different auction.

### Gaps
- *Precision Today* (Berkowitz–Cohen), C.C. Wei's original books, and Meckwell's own notes: not found online in fetchable form.
- No Fantunes source on 1♣ (X) was found. The Wikipedia-linked 2005 Bermuda Bowl card and Daniel Neill's notes were not located. Fantunes 1♣ is described on BBO as "14+ HCP with 5+♣ / 14+ HCP with a 4441 and a red singleton / 15+ HCP balanced", but this is from search snippets only and was not fetched.
- I didn't search WBF/EBL databases (db.worldbridge.org, ecatsbridge) for strong-club cards with a "1♣ (X)" line.

## Q2. When 1♣ is nebulous (can be short), how does opener rebid after XX? Any forcing-pass catch-all or cuebid takeouts?

### Takeaway
One top-level card with a nebulous 2+♣ (Gardenghi–Puerto, Spain, WBF card) plays the same structure as the Strawberry book: "Redouble= 10+ with penalty interest, creates forcing pass until 2NT" and "After XX, later X for penalties". Theirs is a general rule over any takeout double, not one written for 1♣. Swedish (Sundelin–Sylvan) and AUC notes only cover responder's XX, plus the Swedish rule that opener acting over interference shows the strong option (17+). No nebulous-club source with a cuebid as a two-suited takeout was found.

### Cited Findings

**Gardenghi–Puerto (WBF convention card on the Spanish federation site, 2024).** "5 card Major, 1♦ = (4)5+♦ unbalanced, 1♣=2+♣". Opening line: "1♣ … 2+ ♣ 12-14 bal or 18-19 bal or Nat with 4+♣". Transfer responses: "1♦=4+♥;1♥=4+♠;1♠=4+♦ or 4♣333 no 4M;1NT =5♣ XYZ". — [Gardenghi–Puerto WBF card](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2024-06/gardenghi-puerto.docx-3.pdf)
- "OVER OPPONENTS' TAKEOUT DOUBLE — Redouble= 10+ with penalty interest, creates forcing pass until 2NT". — [Gardenghi–Puerto WBF card](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2024-06/gardenghi-puerto.docx-3.pdf)
- "SPECIAL, ARTIFICIAL & COMPETITIVE DBLS/RDLS — After trap pass X for penalties / After XX, later X for penalties and pass is forcing until 2NT / After showing a game forcing hand later pass is forcing and X penalty". — [Gardenghi–Puerto WBF card](https://www.aebridge.com/sites/default/files/2024-06/gardenghi-puerto.docx-3.pdf)
- The card does not say whether the transfer responses stay on over 1♣ (X). It also gives no opener-specific bids after XX: no cuebid, 3NT or jump meanings.

**Sundelin–Sylvan (Sweden), supplementary sheets (JCBL NEC 2002).** Swedish-style club: "-1 ♠ = 4suit, 11-13 OR 5suit, 17+ / -1NT = 11-13, no 4M" after 1♣–1M, i.e. 1♣ = 11–13 balanced or 17+. "1.1 1♣ in comp — If opener acts over interference with anything but a raise or acceptance of an invitation he shows 17+." Over (1♦/1♥) "and (D=♣) we use a transfer structure. 'Accepting' transfer at 1-level shows 3, else SYSON." There is no separate 1♣ (X) XX entry. In the disturbed-2♣ section: "Over (D) … --RD = good (+ later suit=F1)". — [Sundelin–Sylvan supplementary sheets](https://jcbl.or.jp/Portals/0/pdf/nec/2002/swesusyss.PDF)

**An Unassuming Club (AUC; Goldfarb notes).** "a 1C opening that is forcing but not necessarily strong; usually natural". "1C (Dbl) ? — Pass: 0-5 points, any distribution. An immediate double negative. Opener should proceed with caution. Rdbl: 10 points or more, 4 or more clubs, no 4-card major. The usual club positive, but a level lower than usual. 1D: 6-9 points, at least 5 diamonds, no 4-card major. Diamond semi-positive. Opener's rebids are natural; even a pass is possible. 2C: 6-9 points, at least four clubs, no 4-card major. … Other: As they would have been without the double." The notes say nothing about opener after XX. — [AUC notes](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~goldfarb/auc-notes.txt)

**Precision nebulous 1♦ (Timm).** This is the Precision opening that can be short (Timm opens 1♦* with 4-4-1-4 and 5♣4♦). "If the bidding goes: 1♦* - double bid as follows: REDOUBLE shows 5+ hearts asks partner to bid 1♥ / 1♥ shows 5+ spades asks partner to bid 1♠ / 1♠ transfer bid asks partner to bid 1NT / 1NT 8-10 HCP / 2♣ natural 5+ clubs / 2♦ 13+ HCP, diamond raise …". — [Precision Simplified (Timm)](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Precision-Simplified-For-2-1-Game-Force-Players.pdf)
- Timm's general section "Opener's Rebids after a redouble", written in the context of one-of-a-suit openings where XX is 10+: "If the opponents make a runout bid to compete and the opener cannot double the bid for penalty, it is always best to pass and allow responder to bid. … In most situations as opener you should pass a redouble. … However, if opener has a long suit or a strong two-suited hand that is not suited to a penalty double, opener must bid. For example, a reverse would show a strong hand. When the opponents bid over a redouble, a rebid of the suit shows a long suit with a weak hand, a double usually shows 4+ cards in the opponent's bid suit. A bid (or jump bid) of a new suit shows 5-5 distribution and a weak hand. A pass is forcing. If responder passes first and then bids, it shows a stronger hand, usually 5-5 in the two suits and 15+ starting points." — [Precision Simplified (Timm)](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Precision-Simplified-For-2-1-Game-Force-Players.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gardenghi–Puerto is the closest published match to the Strawberry rule: XX = 10+ with penalty interest, a forcing pass, and penalty doubles. Two differences: their forcing pass runs "until 2NT" (a level cap), and no cuebid, 3NT or 18+ meanings are written down.
- Timm's text for limited openings matches the Strawberry style in its main points: pass forcing, double penalty (4+ cards), and an immediate rebid of the suit is weak or competitive. It differs on new suits, where Timm makes an immediate new-suit bid weak 5-5 and Strawberry makes it 18+ natural. Timm's text is not about 1♣, so it should be treated as a parallel only.
- The Swedish rule (opener acting over interference shows 17+) mirrors Strawberry's "Other: 18+, natural", with the minimum hands kept to cheap competitive bids.

### Gaps
- Dutch Doubleton: no write-up giving 1♣ (X) XX continuations was found. BBO threads (e.g. topic 87524 "Dutch Doubleton write-up?") and NBB WHT23 system cards appeared in search, but the NBB card PDFs (bridge.wpaccept01.onsweb.nl) failed to download.
- Italian short club / Lavazza, Neapolitan Club, Nordic clubs: no online source found with 1♣ (X) XX continuations. neapolitanclub.altervista.org appeared in search but was not fetched.
- Swedish "Ultimate"/Svensk Standard: no 1♣ (X) material found. The Nyström–Wrang card on svenskbridge.se failed to download.

## Q3. Which systems answer the double with transfers (XX as a transfer), and what does opener do then?

### Takeaway
Transfers that start with XX are common among Transfer-Walsh players, and Moscito and Timm's nebulous Precision 1♦ also use XX as a step or transfer. But none of the sources defines what opener does after a transfer-XX besides completing or accepting it. Accept/reject rules seen elsewhere (e.g. Sundelin–Sylvan: accepting shows 3) apply to transfers over overcalls, not over doubles.

### Cited Findings
- **Transfer Walsh over 1♣ (X), BBO thread (April–May 2021).** nullve says "system on" is the norm, while some pairs give XX a specific meaning such as "10+" or "4+ D". helene_t suggests redouble = "0-2 clubs (but usually not 0 since we play wjs)" and pass = "3-4 clubs", and mentions two-under transfers (XX = hearts). mikeh: "we take advantage of the double by starting transfers with redouble … We use 1C (x) 1S as a good 2C bid, 1N as 9-11, and 2C as a weaker 2C bid." No post sets out opener's options after a transfer-XX. — [BBO: Transfer walsh question after 1C-(x)](https://www.bridgebase.com/forums/topic/85599-transfer-walsh-question-after-1c-x)
- **Moscito.** XX is a step transfer of the whole response ladder ("xx=1}, 1}=1[, 1[=1N, 1]=2{ etc.") and opener relays. See Q1. — [Moscito 2005](https://www.jackbridge.com/pdf/emoscito.pdf)
- **Timm, Precision 1♦* (X).** "REDOUBLE shows 5+ hearts asks partner to bid 1♥ / 1♥ shows 5+ spades asks partner to bid 1♠ / 1♠ transfer bid asks partner to bid 1NT". There is no super-accept or opener-penalty rule. — [Precision Simplified (Timm)](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Precision-Simplified-For-2-1-Game-Force-Players.pdf)
- **Symmetric Relay.** In relay auctions after a double, "Pass becomes a default relay bid, and Dbl represents the lowest step." The same step-shifting idea as Moscito, from the relayer's side. — [Symmetric Notes](https://nzbridge.co.nz/user/inline/5776/Symmetric_Notes.pdf)
- **Sundelin–Sylvan.** Transfers over overcalls of 1♣, not over a double: "'Accepting' transfer at 1-level shows 3, else SYSON." — [Sundelin–Sylvan supplementary sheets](https://jcbl.or.jp/Portals/0/pdf/nec/2002/swesusyss.PDF)

### Inferences
- When XX is a transfer or step, the penalty idea goes away. Opener's later doubles are takeout (Precision, Dubois) or penalty only once the auction is GF (Moscito). This is the main design trade-off against the Strawberry choice: penalty XX plus a forcing pass, versus constructive or transfer XX plus takeout doubles.

### Gaps
- Sublime and Kokish's transfer-Walsh write-ups: not found online. The 2/1 T-Walsh notes by Michael Goetze ([21twalsh.pdf](https://bridge-tips.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/21twalsh.pdf)) were fetched and have no 1♣ (X) section.
- Pages that could not be fetched or downloaded: zaremba.ch precision_table.pdf, NBB WHT23 cards (bridge.wpaccept01.onsweb.nl), Meep_Meep.pdf (math.mun.ca), svenskbridge.se nystromwrang.pdf.
- I checked other WBF-style cards from search results and found nothing relevant. Paes–Martorell, Sitjar–Bosch–Aranaz (Spain) and Onishuk–De Raeymaeker (Ireland) are natural or standard-club systems, outside scope.
