# What opener's 1♦ means after 1♣ (X) XX

Checked 9 November 2026. The question: in systems where a 1♣ opening usually
denies diamonds, what does opener's 1♦ mean in 1♣ (X) XX (P) 1♦? The evidence
sections record precedents only; the last section is analysis. Nothing here
edits the book.

Inclusion needs a source that establishes both the 1♣ type and a meaning for
XX, and that says something reaching opener's 1♦, even if only through a
generic rule. A card that defines XX and then stops is listed under Excluded.
The 1♣ labels are **Polish-type** (multi-way with a weak balanced range,
including Swedish-style artificial clubs) and **unbalanced ♦** (1♣ balanced or
clubs, with 1♦ unbalanced). Ranges are quoted as printed.

**Conclusion 1.** No source with a values XX names this auction. The only
source that spells out 1♦ under XX is [Sublime][sublime], and its XX is a
diamond transfer. Every statement about a values XX is a generic rule applied
to 1♦, so all 8 of those entries carry ‡.

**Conclusion 2.** The values-XX evidence is three separate rules, and they
describe different things. [Jassem's WJ rule][wj05] (6 of the 8 entries) gives
strength only: below two of the opened suit shows a minimum.
[Stancescu–Draghicescu][stancescu] say “opener bids naturally”, with no length.
[Fools][fools] gives a new suit as “5+ 5(4)+, offensive or min”, which is the
only stated length, and in shorthand.

**Conclusion 3.** No source states 4+♦ as the requirement for this 1♦. Jassem
gives no length, and his 12–14 variant has “no 4-card diamond suit”, which he
does not reconcile with a 1♦ rebid.

**Conclusion 4.** Where XX is a diamond transfer, 1♦ is a limited completion,
often on short diamonds: 5 entries from 4 systems. Sublime has “2–3 ♦s, weak
hand”, with the weak 4+♦ hand bidding 2♦ instead. Petrov–Bauer's and
Mikkelsen–Furunes's acceptance lines are inferences from their uncontested
1♣ rows. These completions say little about a values XX.

**Conclusion 5.** The evidence is thin. The 13 entries come from 7 sources of
authorship: Jassem 6, Sublime 2, and one each for the WJ XXI plus adaptation,
Stancescu–Draghicescu, Mikkelsen–Furunes, Petrov–Bauer and Fools. About 2,300
convention cards and notes produced one explicit statement on opener's action
after 1♣ (X) XX (Stancescu–Draghicescu). Nearly every card that defines XX
stops there.

## XX = values (the group relevant to the book)

### 1♦ = minimum, length unstated

All six are Jassem's. The wording is the point: only the short description and
the English summary reach a 1♦ made after fourth hand passes. The full book and
WJ2000 speak of bidding over the opponent, and their examples all have fourth
hand bidding after 1♠ (X) XX. A 1♦ rebid requires fourth hand to pass, so
applying those two is a stretch.

## Analysis

The book's table at [Opener.md](../src/1C/Competition/Opener.md) is
1♣ (X) XX: P = S/O 4+♣, 1♦♥♠ = NAT 4+, 1NT = BAL MIN 2–4♣, 2♣ = MIN 6+♣, 2♦+ =
STR NAT, 2NT = BAL 18+, 3NT = SOL 7+♣. The book's XX is
[PEN, 9+](../src/1C/Competition.md), so the values-XX group is the fair
comparison.

**Nothing here contradicts the book's 1♦, and nothing confirms it.** A natural
1♦ agrees with Stancescu–Draghicescu and Fools. The 4+♦ threshold has no exact
match. The closest is Fools's '5(4)+', which as read also wants 5+ clubs. That
shape requirement is much narrower than the book's bare 4+♦.

**The WJ rule does not transfer cleanly.** Jassem's 1♦ is a strength rule on a
1♣ whose weak branch denies four diamonds, so its meaning there is unresolved.
The book's weak branch is [12–14 with 2–4 cards in each suit](../src/1C.md) and
can hold four diamonds. The book therefore does not inherit that problem, and
a natural 1♦ on a minimum is coherent there in a way the WJ text does not
explain. Jassem's strength direction does fit the book's section note that a
strain at the lowest level is “to compete”.

**2♣ is where the rules part.** The book's 2♣ is MIN 6+♣. Fools matches
(“rebid suit : 6+suit, min”). Jassem's English says a bid not cheaper than two
of the opened suit shows extras, and his weak variant does not bid clubs on the
second round. The Polish wording leaves exactly two of the suit unaddressed.

**The book's pass has no precedent in these sources.** P = S/O with 4+♣ is a
statement about clubs. Where a pass is defined it means something else:
minimum balanced (Stancescu–Draghicescu), GF any or weak balanced (Fools), or
15+ in the transfer-XX adaptation, inferred from a 1♥ example. No source sets
the 1♣XX sign-off on club length. The book also has 1NT = BAL MIN, whereas
Stancescu–Draghicescu put that hand on pass.

**Transfer-XX completions are not a guide to 1♦ here.** They mostly show
limited hands with short diamonds, because opener is answering a transfer. The
nearest book analogue is 1♣ (X) 1♠! 2♦ = MIN 3+♦, against Sublime's split of
1♦ on 2–3 and 2♦ on 4+. That concerns the book's transfer, not its XX.

Several Polish cards imply that the auction is forcing after XX (Krawczyk's
“forces to our double or 1NT or 2♣/♦”; Sucharda's “forced to 2NT”). The book's
table does not say whether 1♦ is forcing, and these cards do not say what a low
rebid shows, so they settle nothing. Whether 4+♦ is the right threshold is a
judgment or simulation question that this survey cannot answer.
